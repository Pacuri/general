# Cross-Book Application Playbooks

Sequenced engagement playbooks that chain the frameworks from `offers.md` ($100M Offers), `leads.md` ($100M Leads), and `money-models.md` ($100M Money Models).
Each step names the framework it invokes and where it lives. These playbooks sequence and connect — the substance of every framework lives in the referenced file/section; consult it before executing the step.

Trilogy division of labor (money-models.md, Quick reference): Offers = what to sell; Leads = how to find buyers; Money Models = how to get them to buy, in what sequence.

Master ordering for a full engagement: Playbook 1 (diagnose) → 2 (offer) → 3 (money model) → 4 (channels) → 5 (page copy).
This mirrors Market > Offer > Persuasion (offers.md §3) and the money-model rule that each stage must pay for the next (money-models.md, Three Stages).

## Playbook 1 — Diagnose an Existing Business Model

**Inputs you need:** revenue and gross-margin numbers, price list, customer counts, churn %, referral %, CAC by channel, 30-day cash collected per new customer, current offer copy and guarantee, active advertising activities with daily volumes.

1. **Check the market first** — four market indicators (offers.md §3): massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing.
   - Locate the business on the Market > Offer > Persuasion hierarchy (offers.md §3): rate each great/normal/bad, evaluate left to right; a "bad" market stops everything below it.
   - No offer or funnel work pays off until the market verdict is at least "normal."
2. **Check niche commitment and specificity** (offers.md §3).
   - Under $10M/yr → niche down, always.
   - Verify one avatar, one market, no niche-hopping (niche slap rule).
   - Test avatar sharpness against the niche pricing ladder logic — narrower avatar supports up to ~100x price on the same product.
   - Vague avatar = diagnosis finding #1.
3. **Grade the offer on the quality ladder** (offers.md §1): no offer → bad → decent → good → Grand Slam.
   - Then test against the full Grand Slam definition (offers.md §2): attractive promotion + unmatchable value prop + premium price + unbeatable guarantee + money model that pays you to acquire customers.
   - Each missing element is a finding.
4. **Score the offer on the Value Equation** (offers.md §5).
   - Rate Dream Outcome, Perceived Likelihood, Time Delay, Effort & Sacrifice; use the 0/1 comparison scoring exercise against the strongest competing vehicle for the same desire.
   - Check commoditized (price-driven purchase) vs differentiated (value-driven, "sells in a vacuum") (offers.md §2).
5. **Audit pricing against the anti-pattern** (offers.md §4).
   - Is the business doing look-at-market → average → slightly-below? That is pricing for market efficiency.
   - Apply: 99% of businesses need to RAISE prices; no strategic benefit to being second-cheapest.
   - Check whether margin funds excellence via the Virtuous Cycle of Price — low price predicts uninvested clients, worse results, no reinvestment margin.
6. **Run the unit-economics math.**
   - LTGP on gross profit, never revenue (offers.md §2 definitions; leads.md §8).
   - CAC per channel; apply the LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 viability floor everywhere — ads, outreach, employees, affiliates (leads.md §8, §16).
   - Growth cap check: new clients/mo × LTV = max monthly revenue (offers.md §2).
7. **Run the diagnostic forks, in order** (leads.md §8, §12).
   - CAC ≤ 3x industry average → ads are fine; fix the business model (raise LTGP).
   - CAC > 3x industry average → fix the advertising.
   - Engaged leads have the problem AND the money but don't buy → sales problem, not an ads problem. Never fire sales for an ads problem or vice versa.
8. **Check the 30-Day Rule / Client Financed Acquisition.**
   - Does cash collected from a new customer within 30 days exceed CAC + fulfillment cost (money-models.md, Core concepts: 30-Day Rule; leads.md §8, CFA)?
   - If not, the business is in or headed for the bad-money-model death spiral: spend outruns collections → cut ads → float on personal cash → stall (money-models.md, Core concepts).
9. **Inventory the money model against the Four Offer Types** (money-models.md, Core concepts).
   - Which of Attraction / Upsell / Downsell / Continuity exist? One product only = "you barely have a business — you have a front end" (money-models.md, Classic Upsell).
   - Map the business to Stage I / II / III of the Three Stages (money-models.md, Building your Money Model).
   - Check billing hygiene against the continuity micro-plays: cadence (monthly vs 4-week), cancellation policy, second payment method / ACH (money-models.md, Continuity Discount).
10. **Audit lead flow against the Core Four** (leads.md §3).
    - Which of warm outreach / content / cold outreach / paid ads run, at what skill and what volume?
    - The book's standing diagnostic: not enough leads → not doing the Core Four with enough skill or volume.
    - Compare daily volume to the Rule of 100 and the volume lesson ("300 flyers is not a test") (leads.md §9, §15).
11. **Measure the Referral Growth Equation** (leads.md §11).
    - Referral % > churn % = compounding; equal = ads needed to grow; less = the "hamster wheel of death."
    - Check the two causes of missing referrals: product goodwill (value received − price paid) or nobody asks.
    - Roadmap check: 25%+ of customers from referrals is the Level-4 gate before re-scaling ads (leads.md §15).
12. **Locate the funnel constraint** (leads.md §9, BETTER).
    - Compute drop-off at every funnel step; the constraint is the biggest drop-off, and that is the only place testing belongs — one test per platform per week.
    - Calibrate with the worked example: +5 points at the worst step doubled leads; the same +5 elsewhere gained 10-16%.
13. **Check offer fatigue before condemning the offer** (offers.md §13).
    - If a previously working offer decayed, walk the variation ladder first — creative → copy → wrapper → duration → enhancer → money model — before concluding the machine is broken.
14. **Write the findings in fix order:** market → offer → price → money model (30-day cash) → lead volume → constraint tests.
    - This mirrors the leverage order of the trilogy (offers.md §15 application map; money-models.md, five-step evolution sequence).

**Definition of done:** a one-page diagnosis stating market verdict (four indicators), offer ladder position, Value Equation scores, LTGP:CAC per channel with the fork verdict (model vs ads vs sales problem), 30-day cash vs CAC + fulfillment, which of the Four Offer Types and Core Four are missing, referral % vs churn %, the current funnel constraint, and the single highest-leverage fix routed to Playbook 2, 3, or 4.

## Playbook 2 — Construct (or Rebuild) an Offer from Scratch

**Inputs you need:** chosen market/avatar (validated per Playbook 1 steps 1-2), fulfillment cost per additional customer, real capacity limits per week/month, list of client fears/objections and past failure reasons, proof assets (results, testimonials, credentials).

1. **Confirm market and niche before building** (offers.md §3).
   - Four market indicators; pick inside Health/Wealth/Relationships; commit to ONE niche and iterate.
   - Market > Offer > Persuasion: skip this and nothing downstream matters.
2. **Switch to divergent thinking** (offers.md §6).
   - Run the Brick exercise as warm-up (120 seconds; vary size/material/shape).
   - Offer creation has many right answers; Steps 6-8 below explicitly require this mode.
3. **Step 1 — Identify the Dream Outcome** (offers.md §7, Step 1).
   - State the destination in the prospect's terms, not your vehicle — sell the vacation, not the plane flight.
4. **Step 2 — List Problems** (offers.md §7, Step 2).
   - Every obstacle immediately before, during, and after use, in the sequence they'll hit them.
   - Bucket by the four inverted Value Equation drivers: won't be worth it / won't work for me / too hard / takes too long.
   - Expect 30-60+ problems at the worked-example scale. Keep this list — it feeds the money model (Playbook 3).
5. **Step 3 — Turn Problems into Solutions** (offers.md §7, Step 3).
   - Reverse each problem into solution language ("how to" + reversed complaint), then name each solution.
   - Rule: you WILL solve EVERY problem listed — one unaddressed perceived problem can kill the sale.
6. **Step 4 — Create Delivery Vehicles** (offers.md §7, Step 4).
   - For every solution, brainstorm delivery across the cheat-code dimensions: 1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many; DIY / DWY / DFY; live medium; recorded format; response SLA.
   - Run the 10x / 1-10th price test to stretch both directions.
   - Calibrate breadth against the worked grocery-problem brainstorm (~16 vehicles for one problem).
7. **Step 5a — Trim** (offers.md §7, Step 5a).
   - Score every vehicle cost-to-you vs value-to-them: cut high-cost/low-value, then low-cost/low-value; keep low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value.
   - Prioritize high-value ONE-TO-MANY deliverables — the biggest value-to-cost gap.
   - "High value" = passes the four Value Equation questions.
8. **Step 5b — Stack** (offers.md §7, Step 5b).
   - Bundle survivors into one incomparable offer; per-item format: problem → solution wording → sexy name + assigned dollar value + vehicles beneath (worked model: $4,351 value sold at $599).
   - The finished bundle must: solve ALL perceived problems, give you conviction it's one of a kind, make comparison impossible.
9. **Sanity-check fulfillment** (offers.md §7).
   - Place the offer on the Sales to Fulfillment Continuum — the sweet spot sells very well AND is easy to fulfill.
   - Sequence rule: "Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction." Over-deliver first, systematize later with the cash it generates.
10. **Price it premium** (offers.md §4, §5).
    - Widen the value-price gap by raising value, never cutting price; charge up to 100x fulfillment cost as long as it's still a steal.
    - Apply the Virtuous Cycle of Price and the adherence argument ("those who pay the most, pay the most attention").
    - Sequencing rule: price AFTER the stack exists, so the client still gets "money at a discount."
11. **Add scarcity** (offers.md §9).
    - Pick the mechanism: limited supply / limited bonuses / never-available-again.
    - For services pick a cap model: total business cap, growth-rate cap, or cohort cap.
    - Default to honest scarcity: advertise true capacity and current fill level.
12. **Add urgency** (offers.md §10).
    - Pick from the four ethical methods: cohort-based rolling, rolling seasonal (dated promos with countdowns), pricing/bonus-based (the promotion expires, not the service), exploding opportunity.
    - Evidence for deadlines: last 4 hours of a week-long campaign = 50-60% of sales.
13. **Convert the stack into bonuses** (offers.md §11).
    - Apply the 11 bonus rules: benefit-in-title names; relate/what/origin/impact framing; proof; vivid after-picture; dollar value justified; tools & checklists over trainings; each bonus crushes one objection; total bonus value eclipses the core offer; scarcity/urgency on the bonuses themselves.
    - Never discount the main offer — add bonuses to close instead.
    - Consider OPP bonuses from adjacent non-competing businesses (their product free as your bonus + group discount + affiliate commission back).
14. **Craft the guarantee** (offers.md §12).
    - Create-your-own procedure: list the biggest fears/pains/perceived obstacles → reverse them into the guarantee, including time, emotion, and ancillary costs.
    - Pick type by segment: low-ticket B2C → unconditional; high-ticket/B2B → conditional; starting point = service-based or performance/implied guarantees.
    - State via the power formula "If you don't get X in Y, we will Z" — boldly, with a reason why, even if the position is "no guarantee."
    - Decide by guarantee math (1.3x closes at 2x refunds = +23% net), not emotion; guarantee quality alone can 2-4x conversion.
    - Condition the guarantee on the actions that actually produce success (ties to leads.md §11, value fix #3 step 6).
15. **Name it with M-A-G-I-C** (offers.md §13).
    - Use 3-5 of: Magnetic reason why, Announce avatar, Give goal, Indicate interval, Container word.
    - Compliance warning: quantified claim + duration reads as an implied guarantee to ad platforms.
    - Test 2-3 names as an ad campaign; keep the winner as control.
16. **Plan for fatigue** (offers.md §13).
    - Record the variation ladder (creative → copy → wrapper → duration → enhancer → money model) so future refreshes never touch the machine.
    - Rotating multiple proven wrappers is the position of power.

**Definition of done:** a written offer document containing the full stack (each item named + dollar-valued + vehicles listed), price with value justification, chosen scarcity and urgency mechanics, bonus presentation order (ask for the sale before revealing bonuses — offers.md §11), a guarantee in "no X in Y, we do Z" form, 2-3 M-A-G-I-C name candidates with a live name test scheduled, and the fatigue ladder noted. Benchmark: this offer should be capable of carrying the business to its first $100,000 (offers.md §14).

## Playbook 3 — Design a Money Model Around an Offer

**Inputs you need:** the core offer (Playbook 2) with price and fulfillment cost, CAC estimate per channel, current refund rate, the problem list from offer creation (Step 2 — each problem is a future offer slot), billing infrastructure capabilities (cards on file, scheduled charges, ACH).

1. **Set the target before choosing plays** (money-models.md, Core concepts).
   - Minimum bar: more profit from a customer than it costs to get AND service them within 30 days.
   - $100M bar: one customer's 30-day profit funds acquiring and servicing 2+ more.
   - The 30-day window is mechanical: credit-card float finances acquisition if you recoup inside the billing cycle (30-Day Rule).
   - Rationale for stacking all four types: 2x value × 2x customers × 2x speed = 8x growth.
2. **Respect the staged build** (money-models.md, Three Stages + assembly rules).
   - Stage I Get Cash (Attraction) → Stage II Get More Cash (Upsell/Downsell) → Stage III Get Most Cash (Continuity).
   - None of his businesses started past Stage I; a "finished" model launched from zero collapses on top of you.
   - Perfect one offer at a time; measure in quarters, not weeks.
3. **Pick ONE Attraction Offer** — selection logic (money-models.md, Attraction Offers):
   - **Win Your Money Back** — anything people start and quit. Gate: only if baseline refund rate <5%. Budget ~10% money-back claims; pay winnings as store credit applied to a MORE expensive offer, spread over time (never free months up front); build advertising (posts, tags, reviews) into the criteria; make required check-ins upsell slots.
   - **Giveaways** — when you can personally contact all non-winners within 7 days. Promo discount = 10-30% of gross margins; 3-7 day entry; claim within 7 days; use within hours-5 days; second prize to the winner's referrer ≈ 2x leads.
   - **Decoy Offer** — when a stripped free/cheap version pulls leads who then see the premium side-by-side (8/10 took the $399 premium in the proof case). Free = more leads / lower shows; discount = fewer leads / higher shows — use discounts where no-shows are costly.
   - **Buy X Get Y Free** — when free out-pulls discounts (it usually does). Permanently raise prices FIRST; more free than paid; existing-customer bulk-prepay play capped at 10% of the base.
   - **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** — when you can promise a clear yes/no measurable result inside the window. Pay-now discount 20-50% + bonuses; >10% pay-later cancels = overpromised / conditions too easy / price too high.
4. **Pick ONE Upsell Offer, timed by the problem-solution cycle** (money-models.md, Upsell Offers).
   - Mechanism: the upsell sells the solution to the problem the last offer just revealed, at the moment of greatest need — "you can't have X without Y."
   - Why it matters: burger economics — upsells took profit from $0.25 to $3.00 (11.6x); the thing you sell most is rarely where profit lives.
   - **Classic Upsell** — the next problem on the Playbook-2 Step-2 problem list; BAMFAM every meeting; charge for guarantees/warranties (+5-50%).
   - **Menu Upsell** — for multiple products: Unsell → Prescription → A/B choice → Card on file; 'Economist' play (price the A+B bundle equal to B).
   - **Anchor Upsell** — premium at 5-10x presented FIRST; rescue to the main offer on the gasp; always have premium offers ready (asymmetric-loss rule).
   - **Rollover Upsell** — credit past purchases toward a ≥4x more expensive next offer; mandatory companion to Win Your Money Back (absorbs winnings); also the winback and competitor-poach play.
   - Diagnose any failed upsell as wrong thing / wrong time / wrong way before replacing it.
5. **Pick ONE Downsell Offer for the nos** (money-models.md, Downsell Offers).
   - A downsell is any offer made after a "no"; cardinal rule: never the same thing for less.
   - "Too much up front" → **Payment Plan** 7-step ladder (prepay-as-discount → financing/card/layaway → half-and-half → temperature check → thirds → spread → free trial). Present most-cash-up-front first: churn by billing cadence is 10.7% / 5% / 2% monthly/quarterly/annual (Profitwell data).
   - Recurring product requiring customer work → **Trial With Penalty** as the LAST downsell: card + ID mandatory; sell staying-and-paying; explain fees after the card; many small fees over one lump.
   - "Too much overall" → **Feature Downsell**: remove features highest-value-first so customers self-select upward; name the cheapest tier "The Minimum"; barter downsell (discount for reviews/testimonials/intros).
   - Sequencing rule: alternate feature and payment-plan downsells within one sale; temperature check 1-10 routes it (8+ → payment terms; ≤7 → features).
   - Health metric: paid-in-full count must hold after adding plans; if it drops you're leaking full payers into plans.
6. **Pick ONE Continuity Offer — placed LAST** (money-models.md, Continuity Offers).
   - Placement doctrine: run cash-rich Attraction → Upsell → Downsell first, then continuity, then upsell prepaid bulk months at a discount to recover 30-day profit. No successful standalone membership offer observed.
   - Fit test first: ongoing value → ongoing payments only; otherwise it's a payment plan in disguise.
   - **Continuity Bonus** — advertise the bonus, not the membership; bonus worth more than the first payment; standalone priced at 2-2.66x the first month pushes 70-90% into continuity.
   - **Continuity Discount** — free time for a longer commitment; four placements (up front / at end / spread / after first 1-2 payments) with the convert-vs-churn trade-off.
   - **Waived Fee** — setup fee 3-5x monthly, waived for ≥12-month commitment, owed on early cancel; >5% early-cancels = product problem.
7. **Layer the revenue micro-plays** (money-models.md, Continuity Discount).
   - Bill in 4-week cycles: 13 cycles vs 12 months = +8.3% revenue ≈ +41% profit at 20% margins.
   - +3% processing fee; waive it for a second payment method (get ACH — fixes expired/maxed-card leaks).
   - Lifetime discount earned just past the average churn month.
   - Cancellation fee = total discount received; make canceling easy to find; exit interviews save ~1/3 of would-be cancels.
8. **Run the client-financed-acquisition check** (leads.md §8, CFA; money-models.md, 30-Day Rule).
   - Sum expected 30-day cash across the sequence: attraction cash + (upsell take rate × upsell price) + downsell recoveries + continuity first payment/prepay.
   - It must exceed CAC + fulfillment cost of one customer; at the $100M bar, 30-day profit ≥ 2x (CAC + service cost).
   - Short of the bar → add a high-margin upsell inside the first 30 days (CFA worked example: a $100 upsell at 1-in-5 uptake closed the gap) or front-load cash via bulk prepay.
   - Bootstrapped businesses MUST acquire customers at a profit (money-models.md, assembly rules).
9. **Verify the model completes the Grand Slam Offer** (offers.md §2, §15).
   - The money model is part of the Grand Slam definition — payment terms that get you PAID to acquire customers.
   - Performance/implied guarantees from offers.md §12 double as money-model elements.
   - "Simple scales. Fancy fails." — 100 ways to offer one product beats 100 products (money-models.md, assembly rules; mirrors the downsell reframe "100 offers for the same product").
10. **Follow the five-step evolution, no skipping** (money-models.md, Building your Money Model).
    - Customers reliably → pay for themselves → pay for others → max LTV → max ad spend.
    - Build ops and team in parallel: "when your Money Model starts working, your business starts breaking."
    - Raise price in stages until the extra revenue from yeses stops covering the lost nos, then step back one.

**Definition of done:** a documented sequence — one named Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, and Continuity offer, each with price, timing trigger (which revealed problem / which "no"), and its source section in money-models.md — plus a 30-day cash worksheet showing collected cash ≥ CAC + fulfillment (minimum) or ≥ 2x that (target), the refund-rate gate cleared if WYMB was chosen, and a rollout order that perfects one offer per quarter.

## Playbook 4 — Choose and Ramp Lead Channels

**Inputs you need:** the offer (Playbook 2) and money model (Playbook 3), contact list sizes across phone/email/social accounts, honest split of available hours/day vs available budget, LTGP per customer, industry-average CAC if known, capacity ceiling (customers/month you can fulfill).

1. **Define the output correctly** (leads.md §1, §3).
   - Advertising buys engaged leads — people who show interest — not raw contacts.
   - Standing diagnostic: short on leads → not doing the Core Four with enough skill or enough volume.
2. **Build the lead magnet before scaling any channel** — 7-step process (leads.md §2):
   - Pick the narrow problem via the Problem-Solution Cycle: its solution reveals the problem the core offer solves (the bar-pretzels mechanic).
   - Pick type by problem shape: reveal-their-problem (worsens with time) / sample-trial (recurring problem, recurring solution) / one-step-of-many (complex problem).
   - Pick vehicle: software / information / services / physical (3 types × 4 vehicles = up to 12 magnets per problem; rotate to stay fresh).
   - Test the name first — headline is 80 cents of the advertising dollar; test headline → image → subheadline.
   - Make it easy to consume (every format; 2-4x take rates) and make it darn good ("give away the secrets, sell the implementation").
   - CTA: exactly what to do + reasons to act now (scarcity, urgency, "because" reasons).
   - Verify the four jobs of a lead magnet and the CAC math (the free-magnet worked example cut CAC 3x).
3. **Select the starting channel with Core Four decision logic** (leads.md §3).
   - The matrix: warm/cold × 1-to-1/1-to-many.
   - Always start with warm outreach. Then: more time than money → post content; more money than time → cold outreach or paid ads.
   - Paid ads LAST — the other three build the skills and the cash. One method maxed out is enough.
4. **Run warm outreach by the 10 steps** (leads.md §4).
   - Full list (≥1,000 contacts across phone + email + social) → 100 personalized reach-outs/day, up to 3 touches over 3 days.
   - A-C-A on replies (Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask) → offer built on the Value Equation with the who-do-you-know script.
   - First five free (terms: use it, give feedback, killer review if deserved).
   - Pricing ladder: free → 80/60/40% off per 5 → +20% every 5 sales; keep the list warm (9-word email).
   - Benchmarks: 20% reply × 20% take free × 25% convert ≈ 1 customer per 100 reach-outs; ≥4 hours/day on acquisition when starting.
5. **Commit to volume: Rule of 100 / Open To Goal** (leads.md §9, §15).
   - 100 primary actions/day × 100 straight days — 100 reach-outs, or 100 minutes of content/ad creation.
   - Graduate version (Open To Goal): commit to daily outcomes, not hours.
   - Never let effort be the reason it failed: 300 flyers is not a test, 5,000 is; winners go out 5,000/day.
   - First question on any stalled channel: "What's stopping you from doing ten times what you're currently doing?"
6. **Add content (channel #2)** (leads.md §5).
   - Content Unit = Hook (topic from the five sources + headline with ≥2 news elements + native format) / Retain (lists, steps, stories) / Reward (value per second; the audience decides).
   - The audience, not the content, is the compounding asset; volume works (10x output ≈ 10x growth speed).
   - Monetize at Give:Ask ≥ 3:1 or Give Until They Ask; intermittent asks on short-form, integrated on long-form; ask for the lead magnet by default.
   - Scale depth-then-width or width-then-depth.
   - Keep content even if it isn't the main channel: 78% of clients consumed ≥2 long-form pieces before booking.
7. **Add cold outreach when money > time** — solve the three stranger problems in order (leads.md §6):
   - Can't contact them → build the first 1,000-name list (scrapers → brokers → elbow grease).
   - They ignore you → personalization + Big Fast Value (give what people actually pay for; 3x'd take rates in the case).
   - Not interested → volume: automate delivery and distribution, follow up multi-channel ("reach your parents with urgent news" standard), recycle lists every 3-6 months.
   - Benchmarks: 4 engaged leads/100 calls; 3/100 emails; 20/100 personalized DMs; no script tweaks before 100 conversations or 10,000 emails.
8. **Add paid ads last, as an efficiency game** (leads.md §7, §8).
   - Platform passes the four selection criteria; targeting = lookalikes off your consolidated lists + chosen factors.
   - Ad anatomy: Callout → Value → CTA, with angles generated by What-Who-When (8 value elements × status-giver perspectives × timelines).
   - Landing page matched to the ad ("click to close" continuity).
   - Spend by the Three Phases: Track Money before $1 spent → Lose Money (test budget = 2x 30-day customer cash per ad; kill at 1x if zero leads) → Print Money (budget = customers wanted × CAC + 20% pad).
   - Set a permanent 1-10% expected-loss test budget (leads.md §15).
9. **Gate every channel on the economics** (leads.md §8, §16).
   - LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 floor everywhere; aim 5:1-10:1+.
   - Apply both forks before blaming a channel: CAC vs 3x industry average (model vs ads), and problem+money-but-no-buy (sales vs ads).
   - CFA (Playbook 3, step 8) is what removes the cash ceiling on scaling spend.
10. **Scale by More → Better → New, in that order** (leads.md §9).
    - MORE until it breaks (then hold volume while fixing the constraint).
    - BETTER via one test per platform per week at the biggest drop-off; Monday ritual (pick winners, log everything, design next test); 4 failed attempts to beat control (~1 month) → next constraint.
    - NEW: new placements → new platforms → new Core Four activity, last resort.
    - Beware the Size of the Pie Fallacy — one method on one platform is your slice, not the market.
11. **Escalate to Lead Getters when you are the constraint** (leads.md §10).
    - Lead getters are not a fifth activity — you recruit them by running the Core Four; adoption order: referrals → employees → agencies → affiliates.
    - **Referrals (always on):** measure referral % vs churn %; raise goodwill with the six value plays (sell better customers, lower promises until close rates dip, success-action onboarding, faster wins / BAMFAM / the 48-hour window, monthly product-improvement loop, tell them what to buy next); ask with the seven asks (one-sided, two-sided, at purchase, negotiation chip, events, ongoing programs, unlockable bonuses; gift-card combo) (leads.md §11).
    - **Employees:** trigger = personal capacity maxed but the platform isn't (Roadmap Level 2→3, leads.md §15). Recruit via the Internal Core Four; train via 3Ds (Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate — a stranger following the checklist gets your results); economics: cost per engaged lead = payroll ÷ engaged leads; total acquisition cost ≤ 1/3 LTGP (leads.md §12).
    - **Agencies:** trigger = entering a new method/platform with money to spare — skill transfer, never dependency. Learn-then-leave arrangement (6 months, pay extra for the why, train your team, drop to consulting); 10-point selection checklist; run both teams until yours wins consistently (leads.md §13).
    - **Affiliates:** trigger = other businesses hold warm audiences full of your customers ("Who's got my leads!?"). Six steps: find → offer (callout-value-CTA) → qualify (make them customer/expert; cert priced at 10-20% of the average active affiliate's first-12-month earnings) → pay (tiers at 25/50/100% of max allowable CAC) → launch (Whisper-Tease-Shout cadence) → integrate (give magnet / sell magnet / sell core; pay forever, never cap) (leads.md §14). Floor 3:1; aim 5-10:1+.
    - Expectation setting: 3-6 months to crack any new source; ~1 year to scale cold outreach teams or hard paid ads (leads.md §16).
12. **Run it on one page** — One-Page Advertising Plan (leads.md §15).
    - Pick lead type (customers/affiliates/employees/agencies) → Rule of 100 or Open To Goal → advertising checklist → do the daily action until you can pay someone else to do it → repeat with Employees as the new target.
    - "You either did the stuff or you didn't."

**Definition of done:** one primary channel running at Rule-of-100 volume with a tested, named lead magnet; a weekly constraint-test log per platform with a current control; LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 measured on the channel; referral % and churn % tracked monthly; and a written escalation trigger (next channel or next lead getter) tied to the current Roadmap level (leads.md §15).

## Playbook 5 — Translate an Offer into Website / Landing-Page Copy

**Inputs you need:** the finished offer document from Playbook 2 (stack, price, guarantee, M-A-G-I-C name, scarcity/urgency mechanics), proof assets (testimonials, case numbers, credentials), the avatar definition, the money model's entry offer (lead magnet or attraction offer) as the conversion target, the traffic source(s) that will point at the page.

1. **Decide what the page sells** (leads.md §5, §7).
   - The page collects the interested; it doesn't hard-sell — you're gathering contact info from engaged leads (leads.md §7, Step 4).
   - Default ask = the lead magnet (lower risk) or the money model's attraction offer (leads.md §5, monetizing; money-models.md, Attraction Offers).
   - Ad → page continuity is mandatory: carry promise, look, and language through ("click to close"); mismatch burns money (leads.md §7, Step 4).
2. **Headline = the M-A-G-I-C offer name** (offers.md §13).
   - Use the tested control name (3-5 of: Magnetic reason why, Avatar callout, Goal, Interval, Container word).
   - Headline economics: 80 cents of the advertising dollar; ~5x more people read it than the rest; test order headline → image → subheadline (leads.md §2, Step 4).
   - Compliance: quantified claim + duration reads as an implied guarantee to ad platforms (offers.md §13, "I").
3. **Open with a callout that filters** (leads.md §7, Step 3).
   - Verbal callouts: label (group identity; hyper-local beats broad), yes-question, if-then, ridiculous result.
   - Nonverbal likeness: look/talk like the market ("quack like a duck").
   - The page should repel the wrong avatar as deliberately as it attracts the right one (ads are crafted to repel non-buyers — leads.md §7; the "A" of M-A-G-I-C — offers.md §13).
4. **Structure the body on the Value Equation** (offers.md §5) with angles from What-Who-When (leads.md §7, Value).
   - Generate sections from the 8 value elements (4 drivers + their nightmare opposites, carrot and stick) run through status-giver perspectives (spouse, colleagues, rivals) and timelines.
   - **Dream Outcome:** the destination in their terms; frame benefits as status gained in others' eyes ("your golf buddies' jaws will drop") (offers.md §5, Driver 1).
   - **Perceived Likelihood:** certainty is what people pay for — proof, credentials, inclusions/exclusions (offers.md §5, Driver 2).
   - **Time Delay:** speed to the first emotional win; "fast beats free" (offers.md §5, Driver 3); the "I" interval in the headline advertises this.
   - **Effort & Sacrifice:** what they DON'T have to do; DFY > DWY > DIY framing (offers.md §5, Driver 4).
   - Page length inherits the ad rule: long vs short differs only in how many angles you cover — callout and CTA stay constant.
5. **Present the stack as named, dollar-valued items** (offers.md §7 Step 5b; §11).
   - Layout mirrors the Trim & Stack presentation format: problem → solution wording → sexy name + dollar value + vehicles beneath.
   - Apply the bonus rules per item: benefit-in-title name; relate-it / what-it-is / how-discovered / how-it-changes-life framing; proof per item; vivid after-picture; price tag justified; tools & checklists highlighted over trainings; total stack value eclipsing the price (offers.md §11, rules 2-7, 10).
6. **Proof section** (offers.md §5, Driver 2; leads.md §8, §5).
   - Testimonials, case numbers, before/afters — people pay for certainty.
   - Source systematically: UGC/testimonial pipeline as a standing asset; best-performing free content makes the best paid material ~9/10 times (leads.md §8, lessons).
   - The "greatest hits" content list doubles as pre-/post-page nurture material (leads.md §5, lesson 4).
7. **Risk reversal — state the guarantee boldly** (offers.md §12).
   - Power formula on-page: "If you don't get X in Y, we will Z"; always announce your position with a reason why, even if the position is "no guarantee" (an anti-guarantee owned boldly is itself attention-worthy).
   - Match type to ticket: unconditional for low-ticket B2C volume pages; conditional/service for high-ticket B2B (conditions = the success actions).
   - Name the guarantee vividly instead of "30-Day Money Back" (offers.md §12, pro tip).
   - Stack where applicable: unconditional + conditional on sequential outcomes.
8. **CTA logic** (leads.md §2 Step 7; §7 CTA; offers.md §9, §10).
   - Part 1 — exactly what to do: one clear action; "call now," not "don't delay"; clear beats clever; quick and easy (obvious button, short domain, memorable number).
   - Part 2 — reasons to act now: scarcity (real capacity from the offer's cap model, fill level shown — offers.md §9), urgency (deadline/countdown from the chosen method; display countdowns on landing pages for seasonal promos — offers.md §10), and "because" reasons (any reason beats none).
   - Repeat the CTA often down the page.
9. **Continuity device after opt-in** (leads.md §7, Step 4; money-models.md, WYMB meetings-as-offers).
   - Cialdini consistency: "now that you did A, do B to get the most of A."
   - The thank-you step points at the next action in the money model: the attraction offer's first meeting or purchase — which is itself the first upsell slot.
10. **Test like an ad, refresh like an offer** (leads.md §9; offers.md §13).
    - Same testing discipline as any funnel step: one test per platform per week at the biggest drop-off; Monday log; 4 failed attempts to beat control → next constraint.
    - When response fades, walk the offer-fatigue ladder — creative → body copy → headline/wrapper first; never touch the machine.
    - Test 2-3 headline variants exactly as name candidates are tested: keep the control, challenge it (offers.md §13, testing procedure).

**Definition of done:** a page whose headline is the control M-A-G-I-C name; whose sections map 1:1 to the four Value Equation drivers with What-Who-When angles; whose stack items each carry a name + dollar value + proof; whose guarantee is stated in "no X in Y, we do Z" form with a reason why; whose CTA names one action plus real scarcity/urgency and a "because"; whose post-opt-in step routes into the money model's first offer; and a live A/B test log with the current control and the next scheduled test.
