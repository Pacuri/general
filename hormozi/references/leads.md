# $100M Leads (Alex Hormozi) — Reference Distillation

## Quick reference

**Core thesis:** Advertising is "the process of making known" — the master skill that gives endless second chances, because more people knowing about your stuff means more sales even with a mediocre product or weak sales skills. The output of advertising is not leads but *engaged leads* (people who show interest), and there are exactly four ways one person can get them (the Core Four); you scale to $100M by getting other people (lead getters) to run the Core Four for you. If you are short on leads, you are not doing the Core Four with enough skill or enough volume.

## 1. Foundations

### The two ways to grow
Only two ways to grow a business: (1) get more customers, (2) make them worth more. This book covers #1. Get customers by getting leads that are: more, better, cheaper, and reliable (from many places). Rule of thumb: all else equal, double your leads → double your business.

### Lead vs. engaged lead
- **Lead** = anyone you can contact. Nothing more.
- **Engaged lead** = a person who *shows interest* in what you sell (opts in, replies, follows and can be contacted).
- Engaged leads are the true output of advertising. Every process below targets engaged leads, not raw contacts.

### Offer vs. lead magnet
- **Core offer** = your product/service for money. Advertise it first — if it gets enough engagement, you're done.
- **Lead magnet** = a complete solution to a *narrow* problem, free or low-cost, that once solved *reveals the next problem* — which your core offer solves for money (bar pretzels: solve hunger, create thirst, sell drinks).
- Quality bar: valuable enough that you could charge for it; ideally delivers more value than the price of your core offer *before* they buy. A person who pays with time now is more likely to pay with money later.

### The four jobs of a good lead magnet
1. Engages ideal customers when they see it.
2. Gets more people to engage than the core offer alone.
3. Is valuable enough that they actually consume it.
4. Makes the right people more likely to buy.

## 2. Lead Magnets — 7-Step Creation Process

**Step 1 — Pick the problem and who for.** Use the Problem-Solution Cycle: every solution reveals new problems. Choose a narrow, meaningful problem whose revealed next problem is solved by your core offer.

**Step 2 — Pick how to solve it. Three lead magnet types:**
1. **Reveal Their Problem** (diagnosis) — best when the problem worsens with time (website speed test, posture analysis, termite inspection).
2. **Samples & Trials** — brief/full access to the core offer, limited by uses or time — best when the core offer is a recurring solution to a recurring problem.
3. **One Step of a Multi-Step Process** — give one valuable step free, sell the rest — best when the core offer solves a complex problem.

**Step 3 — Pick delivery. Four vehicles:** software (tool/calculator/spreadsheet), information (courses, events, tips), services (free work: audit, adjustment, first coat), physical products (book, sample). 3 types × 4 vehicles = up to 12 lead magnets per narrow problem. Rotate versions to keep advertising fresh and to test.

**Step 4 — Test the name.** Ogilvy: the headline is 80 cents of your advertising dollar; ~5x more people read the headline than the rest. Test in this order: headline → image → subheadline. Better packaging alone can 2x/3x/10x engagement. Method: audience polls, "reply 1 or 2" posts, or direct messages. Mega-winner signal: people ask when they can get it.

**Step 5 — Make it easy to consume.** Offering every format (image/video/text/audio; more times/days/channels for services; easy ordering for physical) raises take/consumption rates 2–4x+ for the same work.

**Step 6 — Make it darn good.** "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation." The 99% who never buy still build or destroy your reputation on the free stuff. People estimate post-purchase value from pre-purchase value received.

**Step 7 — The CTA.** Two parts: (1) exactly what to do (clear, simple, direct: "call now," not "don't delay") and (2) reasons to act now:
- **Scarcity** — advertise your real capacity limits (ethical scarcity: "I can only handle five people per week").
- **Urgency** — deadlines, expiring promos/bonuses.
- **"Because" reasons** — any reason beats no reason (Harvard queue-cutting study); sensible reasons work best.
Rule: don't be clever, be clear. Repeat CTAs often.

### Lead magnet economics (why free lowers CAC)
Core offer profit $10,000. Direct ads: $1,000 per booked call, close 1 of 3 → CAC $3,000. With a free lead magnet costing $25 to deliver: engagement rises so a call costs $75 in ads + $25 delivery = $100/call; 1 in 10 buys → CAC $1,000. Result: 3x cheaper customers, 10:1 return, triple the business on the same budget. A $25 delivery budget out-values every competitor's $0 lead magnet.

## 3. The Core Four

Two audiences × two communication modes = the only four ways one person can advertise:
- **Warm** = people who gave permission to contact them. **Cold** = strangers.
- **1-to-1** (private) vs. **1-to-many** (public). Automation doesn't change the category (an email blast is fast 1-to-1).

| | Warm | Cold |
|---|---|---|
| 1-to-1 | #1 Warm Outreach | #3 Cold Outreach |
| 1-to-many | #2 Post Content | #4 Paid Ads |

Diagnostic: not enough leads → you're not doing the Core Four with enough **skill** or enough **volume**.

**Sequencing:** start with warm outreach. Then: more time than money → post content; more money than time → cold outreach or paid ads. Do paid ads *last* — the other three build the skills and the cash. You only need one method: pick one, max it out (more → better → new). All four compound each other.

## 4. #1 Warm Outreach — 10 Steps

1-to-1 contact with people who know you (calls, texts, emails, DMs). Cheapest, most reliable, lowest leads-per-hour. Advertise your lead magnet or core offer.

1. **Get your list.** Phone contacts + every email account's address book + all social followers/connections. Usually ≥1,000 leads to start.
2. **Pick a platform** — the one with the most contacts.
3. **Personalize your greeting.** Use something you actually know about them as the reason to reach out. No pitch yet.
4. **Reach out to 100 people every day.** Up to 3 contacts per person: once per day for 3 days or until they respond (physical mail: once per week).
5. **When they respond → A-C-A:**
   - **Acknowledge** — restate what they said in your own words.
   - **Compliment** — tie it to a positive character trait.
   - **Ask** — a question steering toward your offer's topic.
6. **Make the offer** after 3–4 message exchanges (or 3–4 minutes live). Build it on the **Value Equation** — maximize (1) Dream Outcome and (2) Perceived Likelihood of Achievement; minimize (3) Time Delay and (4) Effort & Sacrifice. Script skeleton: "Do you know anybody who is (struggle) looking to (dream outcome) in (time delay)? I'm taking on five case studies for free because that's all I can handle. I help them (dream outcome) without (effort/sacrifice). I guarantee (dream outcome) or I work with them until they get it. Does anyone come to mind?" You ask who they *know*, never for them to buy — many volunteer themselves; three winning outcomes (interest, referral, both). One-liner template: "I help (ideal customer) get (dream outcome) in (time period) without (effort and sacrifice) and (guarantee/proof)."
7. **Make it free — the first five.** Terms: free so long as they (1) use it, (2) give feedback, (3) leave a killer review if deserved. Free customers pay three ways: convert to paid, refer paying customers, produce testimonials. If they refuse *free*, the hidden costs (time/effort/sacrifice) are too high or they don't want/believe it — ask "What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?" and fix what the answer reveals.
8. **Start back at the top** — exhaust platform #1, move to #2, etc. 1,000 leads at 100/day = 10 working days; ~a month with follow-ups.
9. **Start charging.** Trigger: when free clients start referring. Ladder: free → "80% off for the next five" → 60% off next five → 40% off next five → keep raising ~20% every five sales until you find the sweet spot. Prices genuinely rise every five = honest urgency.
10. **Keep your list warm.** Give regular value; probe with Dean Jackson's 9-word email: "Are you still looking to [4-word desire]?" — no images, no links. Replies = engaged leads = top outreach priority.

**Benchmarks:** ~1 in 5 reach-outs reply (100 → 20); ~1 in 5 repliers take the free offer (→ 4); ~1 in 4 of those later pays → **~1 customer per 100 reach outs**. $100K math: 1% of list buys a $400 offer → 500 reach outs/week = 5 customers/week × $400 = $2,000/week = $104,000/year. When starting, spend ≥4 hours/day getting customers, first thing in the morning.

**Two limits:** your time, and the finite number of people who know you → add content.

## 5. #2 Post Free Content

### Part I — Making content
Core insight: the content is not the compounding asset — **the audience is**. Volume works (10x output → ~10x audience growth speed).

**The Content Unit** — every audience-growing piece must (a) **Hook** attention, (b) **Retain** it, (c) **Reward** it. The content unit is the smallest material doing all three; long content is content units chained together. Start short — chaining units is the advanced skill.

**Hook = Topic + Headline + Format:**
- **Topics, five sources** (prefer personal experience — only you can tell it): Far Past (life lessons), Recent Past (mine last week's calendar; testimonials/case studies live here), Present (capture ideas the moment they occur; double down on what over-performs), Trending (apply your expertise to what's hot), Manufactured (create the experience to talk about — highest cost, biggest payoff).
- **Headlines:** include at least **two** of: Recency, Relevancy, Celebrity, Proximity, Conflict, Unusual, Ongoing.
- **Format:** match the platform's winning native format first, then tweak.

**Retain = curiosity via embedded questions.** Three vehicles, interweavable: **Lists** (state the count up front), **Steps** (ordered actions, explicit reward), **Stories** (people want what happens next).

**Reward = value per second.** Satisfy the reason they clicked. "There is no such thing as too long, only too boring." Fail states: fewer items than promised, stale items, wrong audience. The metric of reward is audience growth — the audience decides, not you.

### Part II — Monetizing the audience
**Give:Ask ratio.** Mature-platform floors: TV ≈ 3.5:1 (13 min ads per 60), Facebook ≈ 4 posts : 1 ad. These are *monetizing* ratios — the sustainable minimum. Growth mode: dramatically over-give.

**Give Until They Ask (preferred strategy):** never ask; give so much that reciprocity brings people to you. Give in public, ask in private. "The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow down your growth."

**If you must ask — two strategies:**
1. **Integrated** — asks inside each piece keeping the ratio high (60-min podcast with 3×30-sec ads = 58.5:1.5). Place after a valuable moment or at the end; add slots only if growth doesn't slow.
2. **Intermittent** — N pure-give posts, then 1 ask (e.g., 10:1).
Rule: short-form platforms → intermittent; long-form → integrated. Ask for the **lead magnet by default** (lower risk) or the core offer. Core-offer ask template: "I'm looking for 5 (avatar) to help achieve (dream outcome) in (time delay). You don't have to (effort/sacrifice). If you don't get (outcome): money back AND I work with you until you do." After the ask, straight back to giving.

**Two scaling strategies:**
- **Depth-then-width:** post on one relevant platform → post regularly → maximize quality AND quantity (short form up to ~10 posts/day/platform; long form up to ~5 days/week) → add next platform while maintaining → repeat. Pros: compounding, fewer resources. Cons: single-channel risk, no omnipresence.
- **Width-then-depth:** same start, but move to the next platform while maintaining (instead of maximizing) → get on all relevant platforms → then maximize all at once via repurposing. Pros: broader reach faster, repurposing efficiency. Cons: more labor, risk of bad content everywhere.

**Why make content even if it's not your channel:** 78% of his clients had consumed ≥2 long-form pieces before booking a call — content silently nurtures demand every other channel gets credit for.

**7 content lessons:**
1. "How I" beats "How to" — experience can't be argued with; prescription sounds preachy.
2. People need reminding more than teaching — repeat yourself (1 in 5 of his followers didn't know he had a book despite daily posts).
3. Puddles → Ponds → Lakes → Oceans — narrow your topic (what you do, where you do it), win it, expand stepwise.
4. Keep a "greatest hits" master list labeled by problem solved — sales team sends pieces before/after calls.
5. Free content retains paying customers — buyers consume it and count it in their ROI.
6. People don't have shorter attention spans, they have higher standards.
7. Don't pre-schedule posts — manual posting creates the feedback loop that raises quality; a human presses submit.

**Benchmarks:** measure monthly (1) total followers and total reach, (2) growth rate of each (absolute and %). Keep posting and ask cadence constant or output metrics are meaningless. Patience datapoint: 2 podcasts/week for 4 years before Top 100; Top 10 in year five. Keep doing warm outreach + content daily — these two alone can build a six/seven-figure business.

## 6. #3 Cold Outreach

1-to-1 private contact with strangers (calls, email, DMs, door-knocking). Platform-change-proof, competitor-invisible, brutally linear. Expectation: takes ~a year to scale a cold outreach team; hire someone who has done it before, and the operator must track every stat cold.

**Strangers create three problems — solve in order:**

### Problem 1 — You can't contact them → Build a list
Three sources, most to least accessible:
1. **Software scrapers** — subscribe to several; test a representative sample (a few hundred leads each) for accuracy/currency/responsiveness before pulling in bulk.
2. **List brokers** — commission targeted lists from multiple brokers; test samples; keep winners.
3. **Elbow grease** — join groups/communities holding your audience; hand-harvest contacts (prefer contact info usable outside the platform). Freshest, least-contacted leads; slowest.
Goal: first 1,000 names. Time-rich/cash-poor → start with method 3.

### Problem 2 — They ignore you → Personalize + Big Fast Value
- **Personalize** (they don't know you): make the cold reach-out look warm. Research 1–3 pieces of info a friend would know; compliment them on it, ideally showing how it benefited you. Use in subject lines, first messages, first spoken sentences. Batch the research.
- **Big Fast Value** (they don't trust you): blow their minds in under 30 seconds. Give away for free the stuff people *actually pay for*, not "should pay for." Case: replacing a "game planning session" (thinly veiled sales call) with as much free actual service as they could afford **3x'd take rates**. Weak response → keep upping the ante until it's stupid to say no.
- Script hygiene: phone/chat scripts ≤1–2 pages; cold emails ≤ half a page. Do your first 100 conversations or 10,000 emails **before** tweaking.

### Problem 3 — They're not interested → Volume
- **(a) Automate delivery:** pre-recorded voice memos/voicemails/videos, templated emails/texts — record once, send to all.
- **(b) Automate distribution:** robo-dialers, blast sends. Trade-off law: automation sacrifices personalization; the smaller your total addressable list (e.g., 1,000 hedge fund managers), the more you personalize. Allocate 10–20% of effort to brand-new untested tech.
- **(c) Follow up — more times, more ways.** The standard: act like you must reach your parents with urgent news. Multiple channels + speed signal importance. Email first *because* most ignore it — non-response becomes the reason for the next touch ("calling to follow up on my email"). Expect 2–3 conversations before a higher-ticket sale.
- **Recycle the list every 3–6 months** — they didn't see it, timing was bad, or circumstances changed.

**Benchmarks:**
- Phone: 100 calls/day → 20% pickup → 25% of pickups take the lead magnet = **4 engaged leads/day** (~1/hour over 4 hours).
- Email: 100 personalized/day → 30% open → 10% of opens reply = **3 engaged leads (3%)**; target ~3% of list engaged (portfolio example: 4%, ~1/3 of engaged convert → ~1 customer per 100 attempts).
- DM: 100 personalized videos/voice memos (name + one personal line + standard message) → ~20% reply = **20 engaged leads**; qualify with A-C-A.
- Viability: LTGP ≥ 3x cost per customer via outreach (floor; one portfolio company gets 30:1).
- Cost model: rep $15/hr + $50/shown appointment; $3,600 profit/sale; leads $0.10; 200 dials/day → ~2 shows/day; day cost $120 labor + $100 commissions + $20 leads = $240 → $120/show; 33% close → CAC $360 → 10:1. "Then you just add bodies."
- Reverse-engineering: 100 emails = 1 customer; want 100 customers/month → 10,000 emails → 333/day → at 111/person/day, hire 3 senders.

**Seven strategic benefits:** one perfected message (no ad fatigue); invisible to competitors; brutally reliable (input→output equation); immune to platform changes; less compliance pain (FTC only — restricted industries can still advertise); no spokesperson → sellable business; hard to copy (the whole machine is invisible and compounding).

## 7. #4 Paid Ads — Part I: Making an Ad

You pay another business to put your offer in front of their audience — renting eyeballs. Reach is guaranteed, return is not → paid ads are a **game of efficiency**. Narrow the haystack: pick platform → target within platform → craft the ad to *repel* the wrong people → CTA for the rest. Scaling truth: broadening the audience lowers the efficiency ratio but raises total profit ($1K→$10K/$9K profit becomes $100K→$300K/$200K profit). Make the audience as big as possible while still profitable.

### Step 1 — Platform selection (all four must be true)
1. You've used it and gotten value as a consumer.
2. You can target people interested in your stuff.
3. You know how to format ads native to the platform.
4. You have the platform's minimum spend.
Also: consume ads on the platform deliberately — decompose each into callout/value/CTA.

### Step 2 — Targeting (two combinable methods)
1. **Lookalike audiences** — upload your best list; seed in quality order: current + past customers → warm outreach list → cold leads until you hit the platform minimum (later add ad-engagers who didn't buy). Bigger + higher-quality list = better lookalike.
2. **Chosen factors** — age, income, gender, interests, location, time — layered on top. More filters = more efficient but faster audience burn; early specific wins fund later broad scaling. No lookalike capability → target interests.
Action: consolidate all lead lists in one place, separated by source.

### Step 3 — The ad: Callout → Value → CTA

**Callouts (get noticed).** "The purpose of each second of the ad is to sell the next second." His ads got ~20x more effective when most effort went into the first five seconds.
- Verbal: **Labels** (group identity words — "Clark County Moms"; local rule: the more local the better — Americans < Texans < Dallas < Irving); **Yes-Questions** ("Do you wake up to pee more than once a night?"); **If-Then** ("If you run over $100,000/month in ads, we can save you 20%"); **Ridiculous Results**.
- Nonverbal: **Contrast** (bright, moving, attractive — whatever sticks out); **Likeness** (look/talk like the market — "quack like a duck"; broad base → diverse cast; niche → matched cast; mascots endure and never renegotiate; a non-founder spokesperson makes the business more sellable); **The Scene** (visually enact the yes-question: tossing in bed = insomnia; local landmark = locals).

**Value — the What-Who-When framework:**
- **WHAT — 8 key elements** = the 4 value drivers paired with their opposites (carrot/stick):
  1. Dream Outcome ↔ Nightmare
  2. Perceived Likelihood ↔ Risk of inaction / repeating past failures
  3. Time Delay → Speed ↔ slowness of their current trajectory
  4. Effort & Sacrifice → Ease ↔ the work/skill needed without you
- **WHO — status perspectives.** Humans are primarily status-driven; show each element through the eyes of the people who give the customer status: spouse, kids, parents, extended family, colleagues, bosses, friends, rivals, competitors.
- **WHEN — timelines.** Run consequences of buying vs. not through past/present/future, theirs and other people's.
- Angles = element × perspective × time. **Long vs. short ads differ only in how many angles you cover** — callout and CTA stay constant.

**CTA:** spell out the exact next action (click, call, reply "YES," scan). Quick and easy: memorable numbers, obvious buttons, short domains. Layer scarcity/urgency/reasons from the lead-magnet CTA step.

### Step 4 — Get permission to contact them
Simple landing page; test words + image. **Match the ad to the landing page** — carry promise, look, and language through ("click to close" continuity); mismatch burns money. Use Cialdini consistency: "Now that you did A, do B to get the most of A." You're not selling — you're collecting contact info from the interested (= engaged leads).

## 8. #4 Paid Ads — Part II: Money Stuff

Governing idea: "All advertising works. The only thing that differs is how well." Efficiency > creativity; good enough to scale is good enough.

### Three Phases of Scaling
1. **Track Money.** Set up accurate return tracking *before* spending a dollar.
2. **Lose Money.** Expect many small losses, few huge wins. Pattern: $100 × 10 ads = $1,000; nine lose all; one returns $500 (net −$500) → put $10,000 on the winner → $50,000 back. **Ad test budget rule: budget 2x the cash you collect from a new customer in the first 30 days per ad test** (not LTGP) if it's producing leads; **kill at 1x if it gets zero leads.** ($100 30-day cash → let a lead-producing ad spend to $200; kill a lead-less ad at $100.) Judge returns over a long horizon — a year to profitability can be normal, then it pays back fast.
3. **Print Money.** Don't ask "how much should I spend" — ask "how many customers can I handle?" Reverse the budget: 100 customers × $100 CAC = $10,000, **pad +20%** (ads lose efficiency at scale) = $12,000/30 days = $400/day. Commit. "If the number terrifies you, you're doing it right."

### LTGP:CAC — the master metric
- **LTGP** (lifetime gross profit) = all money a customer ever spends minus all cost to deliver ($15 item, $5 delivery, 10 purchases → $150 − $50 = $100). Use gross profit, never revenue-based "LTV" — gross profit is the money that funds everything.
- **CAC** = cost to acquire a customer.
- **The 3:1 rule:** every struggling portfolio company had LTGP:CAC < 3:1; above 3:1 they take off. Two levers: lower CAC (better ads) or raise LTGP (better business model). Prefer both.
- **Diagnostic fork:** compare your CAC to industry average. CAC ≤ 3x industry average → ads are fine; **fix the business model (raise LTGP)**. CAC > 3x average → **fix the advertising**. Finding: CAC between same-industry competitors is much closer than assumed — winners differ on LTGP, not CAC. Costs can only approach zero; LTGP can approach infinity.

### Client Financed Acquisition (CFA)
Fix for profitable-but-cash-poor ads: engineer things so **the customer pays more than it costs to acquire AND fulfill them within the first 30 days** (30 days = interest-free credit-card float). Then cash is never the bottleneck; scale is limitless. Worked example: $15/mo membership − $5 cost = $10 GP/mo; 10-month average stay → $100 LTGP; CAC $30 → 3.3:1 but only $10 back in month one. Fix: add a $100 upsell at 100% margin taken by 1 in 5 new customers → +$20 average GP → **$30 collected in 30 days = CAC covered**; every later $10/mo is gravy. This mechanism scaled every company he started for seven years past $1M/month within 12 months, no outside funding.

### Paid ads lessons
- **Don't confuse sales problems with advertising problems.** If engaged leads have the problem you solve AND the money but don't buy → sales problem (one owner quit "six inches from gold" — ~$30M enterprise-value mistake).
- Best-performing free content makes the best paid ads ~9 times out of 10; systematize UGC (testimonials/reviews) as a standing ad pipeline.
- "If you say you suck at something, you will probably suck at it" — the tech excuse cost him four years; reversed in four hours.
- Onboarding: search "how to place a [platform] ad," spend $100, place one. Set a monthly loss budget you can afford — you're learning, not earning.
- Do paid ads **last** of the Core Four.

## 9. More → Better → New (scaling any channel)

**Order of operations:** MORE of what works → BETTER via testing when it breaks → NEW places/ways only when returns from more/better fall below what a new placement/platform/method would yield.

**Size of the Pie Fallacy:** one method + one platform + one audience is your slice, not the market ($30K/mo on one platform is not saturating a $15.1B industry; a same-niche peer spent $30K/week across four platforms).

### MORE — The Rule of 100
**100 primary actions per day, for 100 straight days.** His one promised guarantee in the book. Applied:
- Warm outreach: 100 reach outs/day (email/text/DM/call).
- Content: 100 minutes/day creating; publish ≥1 piece/day (more as you improve).
- Cold outreach: 100 reach outs/day (automate; expect lower response).
- Paid ads: 100 minutes/day making ads + run them 100 days straight at the reverse-engineered daily budget, aiming for CFA.
Do more until it breaks (CAC too high) — then the constraint becomes obvious; hold volume while fixing it. First diligence question on any business: "What's stopping them from doing ten times what they're currently doing?" Sometimes nothing — just do more.

### BETTER — constraint testing
Every funnel step is a drop-off point; the **constraint** is the step with the biggest drop-off — test there. Worked example (30% opt-in / 5% apply / 50% schedule): +5 points on opt-in = 1.16x leads; **+5 points on apply (5→10%) = 2.0x leads**; +5 on schedule = 1.1x. Always test the constraint.
**Protocol: one test per platform per week.** Four reasons: isolates cause; steps interact (an upstream change can hurt downstream); forces prioritization of high-impact tests; a week gives enough data. **Monday ritual:** (1) pick winners per platform, (2) log every result in a running test log, (3) design the next test to beat the current control. **If you can't beat the control in 4 tries (~1 month), move to the next constraint.**

### NEW
Expansion order (choose by most leads per unit of work): **new placements** on the current platform → **new platforms** (same format elsewhere) → **new Core Four activity** (last resort). Then run more/better on the new thing. Repeat.

## 10. Lead Getters — Overview

**Lead getters** = other people who do the Core Four on your behalf. Leverage ladder: do it yourself (high work/low leads) → one lead getter (low/low, high leverage) → many lead getters (high/high) → **a lead getter who recruits lead getters** (low work/high leads — the $100M machine).

Key structural insight: lead getters are not a fifth activity — you *get* them by running the Core Four (they start as engaged leads), then they run the Core Four for you, forever. Ideally every lead becomes a lead getter. Four flavors: **Customers** (referrals), **Employees**, **Agencies**, **Affiliates**. Natural adoption order: referrals → employees → agencies → affiliates. Expectation: cracking any new lead source takes 3–6 months even for a veteran; pick a strategy and stick with it for ~a decade.

## 11. Lead Getter #1: Customer Referrals

**Why referrals dominate:** referred customers are worth more (buy more expensive things, more often, pay cash upfront → higher LTGP) and cost less (CAC ≈ 0). At 4:1 LTGP:CAC, if every customer brings two friends, the effective ratio becomes ~12:1. Core Four channels are linear; referrals are exponential (1→2→4→8). "Nothing scales like word of mouth."

**Referral Growth Equation:** growth = referrals in − churn out.
- Referral % > churn % → compounding growth with zero other advertising.
- Equal → advertising needed to grow.
- Less → advertising just to stand still (most businesses — the "hamster wheel of death").
Baseline action: measure your referral % and churn % before anything else.

**Two reasons businesses lack referrals:** (1) the product isn't as good as they think; (2) they don't ask.

### Fix #1 — Goodwill: give more value
**Goodwill = value received − price paid.** Goodwill drives word of mouth. Two levers: cut price (bad — finite, unprofitable) or add value (the play). Self-audit: "Why are my customers too embarrassed to tell everyone they know about my product?"

**Six ways to give more value (maps to ad anatomy):**
1. **Callouts → Sell better customers.** Find what your lowest-churn/best-result customers share; aim all targeting and messaging at only that profile. (PR firm case: re-aiming at the best 15% broke a multi-year plateau, cut ad costs, started clockwork referrals.)
2. **Dream Outcome → Set better expectations.** You set expectations, so set them low enough to overdeliver. Rule: **lower your promises until close rates start to drop — then stop.**
3. **Likelihood → Get more people better results.** Six steps: (1) survey customers to find who got the best results, (2) interview them for what they did differently, (3) extract the common actions, (4) force new customers to repeat those actions, (5) measure improvement in average results (speed + outcome), (6) **tie your guarantee's conditions to those success actions.** (Gym Launch: owners who ran ads AND made a sale within the first 7 days had 3x LTGP → mandatory onboarding.)
4. **Time Delay → Faster wins.** Five tactics: (a) deliver seven small things at intervals rather than one big thing (7 kept promises, not 1); (b) share updates constantly — updates ARE wins; (c) **the first 48 hours after purchase form the lasting impression** — pack that window with set-and-met expectations; (d) **BAMFAM** (Book A Meeting From A Meeting) — the customer must always know the next touchpoint; (e) pad all timelines +50% so you always deliver early, never late.
5. **Effort & Sacrifice → Keep making the product better.** Monthly loop, forever: (1) mine service data/surveys/reviews for the #1 most common problem, (2) design the fix — seed it with feedback from customers who succeeded despite the problem, (3) improve the product, (4) release to a small group of struggling customers, (5) solved → roll out to all; not → back to step 2, (6) next most common problem; repeat.
6. **CTA → Tell them what to buy next.** Satisfied customers want to buy more; if you don't sell them, someone else will. Ask after each big win; customers who fall off don't refer. (Some customers who never referred the entry product referred the expensive one they bought later.)

**Thought experiment:** all future customers must come from your single remaining customer — how would you treat them? Write it out, then actually do it.

### Fix #2 — Ask. Treat the ask as an offer
Show the referrer the value *they* get. Canonical results: Dropbox two-sided free storage → 39x in 15 months; PayPal $10 + $10 credits → 1M users within 2 years, 100M users six years after that.
Program components: how you give the incentive, what you incentivize with, how you ask.

**Seven ways to ask:**
1. **One-sided benefit** — pay your full CAC to the referrer OR the friend. Ask at the moment of purchase for a real **3-way introduction** (call/SMS/email — never just a name and number). CAC $200 → $200 check to referrer, or $200 off for the friend.
2. **Two-sided benefit** (Dropbox/PayPal model) — split CAC: half cash/credit to referrer, half discount to friend ($500 program, $200 CAC → $100 cash + $100 off, up to 3 friends). Best performer in his local businesses.
3. **Ask right when they buy** — collect names/numbers on the contract/checkout page. Script: "People who do our program with someone else tend to get 3x the results. Who else could you do this with?" (A rep doing only this got half his sales from referrals.)
4. **Referral as negotiation chip** — trade discounts for 3-way intros instead of dropping price unconditionally. If a full-price customer objects: offer them the same deal.
5. **Referral events** — 1–4 week promos: Bring-a-Friend, Spouse Challenge, Accountability Buddy, Coach Challenge (staff-customer teams); sell the together-success stats.
6. **Ongoing referral programs** — bake "do it with others" messaging into all content/outreach/ads permanently (+33% signups; 250,000 of 1,000,000 event tickets referred).
7. **Unlockable referral bonuses** — non-cash tiers for referring + leaving a testimonial: VIP bonuses, courses, status, merch, premium support, extra hours.
**Combo play — the gift card:** give every buyer a gift card worth 1/3 the program price, valid only for a friend, expiring in 7–14 days; pair with a 3-way intro and a photo of the card with the friend's name on it. Variant: sell the gift cards at 90% off — you get paid to acquire customers.

**Psychology:** a referral risks the customer's goodwill with their friend. They refer when expected benefit (incentives + confidence the friend wins) outweighs relational risk. Raise benefits with the 7 asks; lower risk with the 6 value plays.

## 12. Lead Getter #2: Employees

Lead-getting employees run the Core Four inside your business. Management trade: 40 hours of doing ↔ 4 hours of managing; stacks (200 hours of work ↔ 20 hours managing ↔ 4 hours leading a manager). Wealth rule: a profitable business that needs you is a risky job worth ~nothing to a buyer; the same business running without you is a sellable asset. "You get rich from what you make. You become wealthy from what you own."

**Mindset prerequisites:** replace "no one can do it like me" with "if you want it done right, get someone to spend all their time doing it"; "if I can do it, someone else can do it better"; "everyone is replaceable, especially me."

**Internal Core Four — recruit talent exactly as you advertise for customers:**
| Customer channel | Employee equivalent |
|---|---|
| Warm outreach | Ask your network |
| Cold outreach | Recruiting |
| Post content | Post job openings |
| Paid ads | Promote job postings |
| Customer referrals | Employee referrals |
| Affiliates | Associations, guilds, listservs |
| Agencies | Staffing firms |

**Hiring-bottleneck diagnostic (pattern):** trace missed targets up the chain until you find the constraint. Case: missed cold-outreach goals ← rep churn (below industry average — not the issue) ← hiring 1 of 4 candidates ← 1 qualified candidate per 10 screening interviews = 40 interviews/hire → the constraint was 1-on-1 screening → switched to **group interviews** (screen only for red flags + work ethic + basic social skills; teach the rest) → hiring outpaced churn in 6 weeks; sales doubled. Hiring philosophy: for ground-level advertising roles, HOW you train matters more than WHO you pick; reserve pickiness for expensive C-suite hires.

### 3Ds Training Model
1. **Document** — write the checklist of exactly what you do (record yourself over multiple shifts). Validate: can you do an A+ job following only the checklist?
2. **Demonstrate** — do it in front of them step by step; wherever they stop you or slow down, fix the checklist.
3. **Duplicate** — they do it in front of you from the same checklist; wrong outcome exposes checklist gaps; iterate until they nail it.
Bar: a stranger following the checklist should get your results.
Training principles: confusion means the step is too complicated (probably several steps in one); competence ≠ performance ("slow, then smooth, then fast" — sometimes they just need reps); grade direction-following over outcomes (then bad outcomes indict the directions, which you control); praise every correct step; never punish during training; one piece of feedback at a time; on any sudden performance dip, a step got silently dropped — retrain and reward it.

### Employee lead economics
- **Cost per engaged lead = total payroll ÷ total engaged leads** ($100,000 / 1,000 = $100/lead).
- CAC = cost/lead × leads per customer ($100 × 10 = $1,000); at $4,000 LTGP → 4:1.
- Real datapoint: ~30,000 engaged leads/month from a ~$100K/month content team = ~$3.33/lead, no ads, no outreach.
- **Diagnosis rule:** CAC ≤ 3x industry average → good; push LTGP. CAC > 3x → ask "Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve AND the money to spend?" No → advertising problem. Yes but too few → advertising problem. Yes but not buying → **sales problem**. Never fire sales for an ads problem or vice versa. Solvency floor: total acquisition cost ≤ 1/3 of LTGP.

## 13. Lead Getter #3: Agencies

**The agency trap (10-step cycle to avoid):** excitement → valuable-feeling onboarding → "best" senior rep assigned → some results → senior rep silently moved to the newest client → junior rep degrades results → complaint → senior briefly returns → results still suffer, eventually cancel → hire the next agency and repeat.

**The right way — agencies as skill transfer, not dependency.** Use agencies for exactly two things: **learning new methods** and **learning new platforms** (they've already made the expensive mistakes). Only with money to spare; no money → learn by trial and error.

**Opening script (set terms up front):** work with me for 6 months so I can learn how you do it; I'll pay extra for you to explain why you make the decisions you make; then I train my team; once they're good enough we switch to a lower-cost consulting arrangement for when we hit problems. Most agencies accept; negotiate — at some price it works for both.

**Two-agency strategy:** hire one "good enough" agency to learn a platform's ropes + one elite agency (he paid 4x) to learn depth; run your own team in parallel; when your results beat theirs consistently, drop to consulting-only, then cut loose ("until you feel like you're teaching them"). Expect a period of paying both agency and team — that's tuition. Learning curve: ~1 year the first time, under 6 months with reps.

**10-point agency selection checklist:**
1. Someone you know got good results with them (the best grow by word of mouth).
2. Recognizable companies got results with them.
3. They have a waiting list.
4. Clear sales process that sets realistic expectations.
5. No short-term hacks — long-term strategy with explicit timelines for setup, scaling, results.
6. They tell you exactly what they need from you, when, and how they'll use it.
7. Regular meeting schedule + several update channels.
8. Plain-language updates with clear cost-vs-results tracking.
9. They make a good offer (evaluate on the value equation).
10. They're expensive — all good agencies are expensive, but not all expensive agencies are good.

**Next steps:** decide if an agency makes sense now → talk to many, don't be cheap → use the agreement framework → set a hard learning deadline → run both teams until yours wins consistently → switch to discounted consulting, then cut loose.

## 14. Lead Getter #4: Affiliates

**Definition:** an affiliate = an independent business that advertises your stuff to *their* audience for money, free stuff, or both. Offer them a money-making opportunity, not a product. **Leverage math:** selling 10 customers/month at $10K yourself = flat $100K/mo ($1.2M/yr); selling 10 affiliates/month who each bring one $10K customer monthly = +$100K/mo of new recurring revenue every month ($7.8M in year one, climbing).

### Six Steps to an Affiliate Army

**Step 1 — Find ideal affiliates.** Ideal = a business with a warm audience full of people like your customers. Ask of your best customers: What do they buy → who provides it? Where do they go → what's nearby? What do they like to do → who provides it? (B2C: who employs them?) Summary question: "Who's got my leads!?" Categories: softwares, products, equipment, services, groups they belong to, events they attend — a business in multiple categories is a prime affiliate.

**Step 2 — Make them an offer** (same ad anatomy: callout → value → CTA). Callout options: the owners ("ATTENTION SPA OWNERS"), their customers, the results they promise, the products they sell, or your own customers ("Do you know anyone who owns a spa?"). Value skeleton: more money from current customers + more leads (dream outcome); high chance since their customers already want it (likelihood); no building, delivering, or supporting (effort); start selling tomorrow (speed).

**Step 3 — Qualify them.** Engineer terms to force a fast first win by making them **invest** ("if they pay, they'll pay attention"). Two mechanisms, combinable:
- **Make them a customer** — must buy/use the product to keep affiliate status; affiliates who invest more, sell more.
- **Make them an expert** — paid onboarding/training certification (product purchases can credit toward it); the fee covers acquisition cost and funds proper onboarding.
**Pricing rule: charge 10–20% of what the average ACTIVE affiliate earns in their first 12 months** (avg $40K/yr → $4,000–$8,000 certification). Too low = not invested; too high = too few. Calibrate: not enough sign-ups → lower the commitment; not enough follow-through → raise it (or raise the minimum every 5 sign-ups, per the warm-outreach ladder).

**Step 4 — Figure out what to pay them.**
- **What for:** new customers and repeat customers; later (with tracking) any upstream step that reliably converts (lead-magnet downloads, appointments).
- **How much:** derive from your **maximum allowable CAC**. Example: $200 single-use product, $40 fulfillment → $160 available; at 3:1 LTGP:CAC the business keeps $120, affiliate max = $40.
- **Three-tier payout structure** (on the $40 max):
  - Tier 1 = 25% of max CAC ($10) — anyone who agrees to initial terms (signs up, buys product/cert).
  - Tier 2 = 50% ($20) — once they **activate** (finish certification, hit posting/outreach quotas, run a launch).
  - Tier 3 = 100% ($40) — sustained performance (e.g., maintain 5 subscription customers/month).
- Side effect: a 20/20/60 tier mix gives a $30 blended payout vs. $40 max → LTGP:CAC improves 3:1 → 4:1; the surplus funds contests, affiliate ads, rising-star incentives.

**Step 5 — Get them advertising: Launch (Whisper → Tease → Shout).** Do all the prep for them — plug and play. Affiliates pre-sell to their audiences, then sell on launch day. (This is the general-purpose launch playbook for anything.)
- **Whisper** (= callouts): curiosity; keep the product mysterious; show behind-the-scenes; can start years out — longer apparent build time = higher perceived value. Cadence: every 4–6 weeks until 60 days out; every 2–3 weeks until 30 days out.
- **Tease** (= value elements): reveal the product, publish the launch date, present value via What-Who-When. Cadence: 1x/week until 14 days out; 2x/week until 3 days out.
- **Shout** (= CTA): explicit actions + launch-only bonuses, scarcity, urgency, "first ones" guarantees. Cadence: ≥2x/day from 3 days out; on launch day every few hours until 2 hours out; then every 30 minutes until launch.

**Step 6 — Keep them advertising: Integration** (three modes, easiest → hardest):
1. **They GIVE your lead magnet away** with every purchase of their stuff — raises their offer's value at no cost; you upsell the leads (free massage with every training package; free posture assessment; free step one of your plan; gyms gave a free nutrition consult, supplements upsold there).
2. **They SELL your lead magnet and keep the cash** — all profit, no work for them; letting them keep 100% makes them send more; you monetize on the core-offer upsell behind it.
3. **They SELL your core offer directly** — bigger share of your LTGP; you only deliver; can become their whole business.
Split options: upfront cash only, all cash for a period, or all cash forever — prefer **paying forever and never capping payouts** so affiliates stay motivated to keep your customers. Steady-state mix he uses: mode 1 twice per year as big events + mode 3 ongoing.

### Affiliate economics
Compare **cost to acquire an affiliate** vs. **gross profit of all customers the affiliate sends**. Example: affiliate CAC $4,000; average affiliate sells $10,000/mo × 12 months = $120,000; 75% gross margin → $90,000 GP; pay affiliate 40% of GP = $36,000 → $54,000 kept → **12.5:1**. Floor 3:1; aim 5:1–10:1+. If under 3:1, three levers: (1) lower affiliate CAC (better ads/offer/sales), (2) activate more affiliates (better launch process), (3) make each worth more (better integration).

**Bootstrap sequence:** advertise the affiliate offer until you have 10–20 affiliates → debug offer/terms/launch/integration with them → turn their results into your affiliate-recruiting lead magnets → scale.

**Reproducible case patterns:** tax-prep ($50M, zero paid ads): affiliates = entrepreneur trainers; magnet = free LLC setup (deliberately high-cost); launch = seminars to affiliate audiences; monetize = phone upsell of bookkeeping/tax. Supplements: 4,000 gyms as affiliates; free 28-day challenge launch; nutrition orientation on every new membership; ~14 sales/gym/month × $200 average order. Chiropractor: gym hosts a 3-hour posture workshop (gym keeps 100% of $29–$99/head), chiro soft-pitches; integrate 1–2 free adjustments into every new membership; repeat across 30 gyms.

**Lead getter strengths recap:** referrals = cheapest exponential growth; employees = your direct leverage; agencies = skills you keep forever; affiliates = eventually fully self-operating.

## 15. Get Started — Execution System

### Test-budget principle
Permanently allocate a fixed percentage of advertising budget (1%, 5%, or 10%) to test new campaigns/channels/pages/crazy ideas with **no expected return** — it's tuition. "You either win or you learn." Meta-pattern: test until something works → massive action → double down until it breaks → test for the next thing.

### The volume lesson
300 flyers is not a test. Standard: test with 5,000 flyers; a winner goes out 5,000 *per day*, every day, for a month. Print response benchmarks: 0.5% = decent; 1% = winner. Most people do a tiny fraction of the required volume ("100 people over six weeks" is 1/42 of the prescription — it's 100 per day, not 100 over time). Never let effort be the reason it failed.

### Rule of 100 / Open To Goal
- **Rule of 100:** 100 primary actions/day × 100 straight days (see section 9 for per-channel application).
- **Open To Goal** (the graduate version): commit to work until a specific number of daily *outcomes* is hit, regardless of hours (5 new members/day — done by lunch or at hour 18). Replaces "do your best" with "do what is required."
- Supporting habit stack: wake 4–5 a.m. (= sleep early); straight to work, no rituals; no meetings until noon — one long uninterrupted daily work block sized to the daily goal.

### One-Page Advertising Plan (5 steps)
1. Pick the type of engaged lead to get: Customers, Affiliates, Employees, or Agencies.
2. Pick Rule of 100 or Open To Goal; commit to daily advertising actions.
3. Fill out the advertising checklist for that daily action.
4. Do the daily action until you can afford to pay someone else to do it.
5. Return to step 1 with **Employees** as the new target lead type; repeat 1–4 until staffed; then scale again.
One page, ~5 minutes to fill: "you either did the stuff or you didn't."

### The Roadmap — 7 levels of advertisers
1. **Friends know what you sell** — one offer, one avatar, one platform. Primary action: warm outreach.
2. **You consistently tell everyone you know**; you know your exact inputs per engaged lead; personal capacity maxed. Primary: max warm outreach + post as much content as you can consistently.
3. **Employees help you advertise** — you've maxed yourself, not the platform. Primary: hire people to advertise profitably on your behalf (his first hires: videographer + media buyer).
4. **Product good enough for consistent referrals** — target **25%+ of customers from referrals**; ship customer-feedback-driven product updates (his cadence: every two weeks) + a strong-incentive referral program; THEN re-scale advertising with a bigger team. Most common failure point: letting the product slip while scaling ads.
5. **More places, more ways, more people** — expand audiences on your best platform → all placements/media types → then a new platform, lead getter, or Core Four activity. Primary: profitable advertising via ≥2 methods on multiple platforms.
6. **Hire killers** — veteran executives with problem-specific experience and strong incentives ("expand the pie") who grow channels without you.
7. Reserved (to be written after $1B).
Timeline for someone who knows what to do: **5–10 years** to the $100M machine.

**$100M machine end-state:** media team scaling content in all media on many platforms; regular warm-audience offers for customers and affiliates; launches instantly profitable to a ravenous audience; teams scaling profitable paid ads on multiple platforms; a cold outreach team; an affiliate manager launching and integrating affiliates; recruiters feeding lead getters; a product so good ~1/3 of customers refer; an executive team driving it without you; more engaged leads than you can handle.

### Closing operating rule — the Many-Sided Die
Advertising attempts are dice rolls; every success permanently improves your odds (each green rolled turns a red side green). With enough rolls, starting skill/luck stops mattering. Two guarantees: (1) more rolls = better; (2) quit = lose. "You cannot lose if you do not quit."
