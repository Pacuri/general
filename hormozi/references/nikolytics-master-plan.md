The entry offer is the **$25,000 Blueprint** (spec + prototype sprint, 100% credited toward a build within 60 days). The anchor is the **$1,500,000 Founding Partner Build**, one client per year.

**The channels.** Warm before cold, free before paid. **100 personalized warm reach-outs per day for 100 straight days starting 2026-07-08**, plus 100 minutes of daily content narrating build work that already exists. Three lead magnets feed one ladder (Teardown → Blueprint → Sprint). Referral asks go out week 1 at $10,000 per closed build. ## Part 2 — What the Market Research Found

Run 2026-07-03 through a deep-research harness: 104 agents, 5 search angles, adversarial 3-vote verification on every claim. Refuted claims were deleted, not softened — everything below survived verification.

### What was researched

Seven questions: (1) demand reality — do funded seed-to-Series-B startups outsource core platform builds, at what rate, with what triggers (failed hires, no-code ceilings, launch deadlines)? (2) the 2025-26 competitive landscape of rapid/fixed-price build shops — real prices, timelines, guarantees; (3) pricing benchmarks — is $250k fixed defensible in the $50k-$500k band? (4) does anyone already own "30 days, fixed price, you own everything"? (5) buyer behavior — how funded founders evaluate dev partners, objections, where they look; (6) what actually works for agency client acquisition via LinkedIn in 2025-26; (7) evidence on paid scoping sprints ($5k-$25k) as entry offers and their conversion to full builds.

### The verdict in one paragraph

"30 days, fixed price, you own everything" is **not** open whitespace — it's occupied at lower tiers and undercut on speed. $250k fixed is arithmetically defensible but implies an effective ~$240-$360/hr when compressed into 30 days, so the pitch must sell the outcome, never hours. Cold LinkedIn to US founders is a below-average-response channel. The paid-scoping-with-credit mechanic is proven but the $25k price sits at the top of the observed range. And the plan's most important assumption — that funded startups outsource core builds at meaningful rates — survived verification with **no** solid evidence behind it.

### The eight findings

**Finding 1 — the positioning is already occupied.** (Confidence: high. Votes: 3-0, 3-0, 3-0.)
AppMakers USA runs exactly this frame: 30-day MVP, fixed-price quote after a **free** scope call ("no hourly billing, no surprise invoices"), "100% of the code, the IP, the assets... from the first commit", framed as "AI-accelerated engineering", not "vibe-coding". Credible: Clutch Premier Verified, 98 reviews, 5.0 rating, clients including CVS and the NFL. Two consequences: the $25k paid Blueprint competes directly against free scoping, and AI-accelerated speed positioning is an established frame. Qualification: AppMakers benchmarks against $80k-$200k+ traditional engagements and positions **cheaper** — the overlap is on the speed/ownership/fixed-price pillars, not the $250k band.
Sources: https://appmakersla.com/services/30-day-mvp/ | https://clutch.co/profile/appmakers-usa

**Finding 2 — the 30-day timeline is already undercut.** (Confidence: high. Votes: 3-0, 3-0, 3-0.)
Maxiom Technology: fixed price from $10,000, 14-day average time to launch, $5k-per-sprint continuation packages, full source code ownership and portability. MVPExpert independently advertises 14-day launches at $4,990. Qualification: the $10k scope is a lean MVP (up to 5 features), so segment overlap with a $250k platform build is partial — but the timeline undercut is literal.
Sources: https://www.maxiomtech.com/mvp-development/ | https://mvpexpert.com

**Finding 3 — a low-end tier floods the category.** (Confidence: medium. Votes: 2-1, 3-0.)
AI-accelerated MVP studios saturate "fixed price + fast + full ownership": HouseofMVPs $7,499 in 14 days with "100% Code Ownership... transferred to you on launch day"; RocketMVP $3,999 in 21 days; SpeedMVPs $5k-$50k+ in 2-4 weeks. The advertised $7k-$25k band is 10-35x below Nikolytics' price for a superficially similar timeline promise. Caveats from verifiers: these are thin MVPs, not seed-to-Series-B platform builds (premium studios in this segment run $30k-$80k over 8-16 weeks), and the anchor listicle is self-promotional (HouseofMVPs ranks itself #1). What this establishes: category crowding and price-anchor risk, not scope-equivalent competition.
Sources: https://houseofmvps.com/blog/mvp/top-ai-mvp-development-agencies-2026 | https://houseofmvps.com/ai-mvp-development | https://rocketmvp.com | https://speedmvps.com

**Finding 4 — $250k is defensible, the 30-day compression is the strain.** (Confidence: medium. Votes: 3-0, 3-0.)
US dev shops on Clutch charge $100-$149/hr (offshore: $25-$49), so $250k implies 1,700-2,500 hours — a plausible 4-6 person intensive engagement. But Clutch's verified-review average project is ~$132,480 over ~13 months (~$10,209/month), so $250k sits upper-middle-to-upper-tail, and 30-day delivery is ~13x faster than the typical reported timeline. Fitting 1,700-2,500 hours into 30 days with 4-6 people is not possible — the 30-day promise implies an effective **~$240-$360/hr**, above the standard US band. The pitch must sell outcome and speed value, never hours. Caveats: Clutch averages blend heavy offshore volume (most common project range $10k-$49k) and review-based durations skew long, so the 13x comparison is directional. Verifiers corroborated US agency rates of $70-$200+/hr via FullStack Labs and others.
Source: https://clutch.co/developers/pricing

**Finding 5 — comparison shoppers see entry prices far below yours.** (Confidence: medium. Votes: 3-0, 3-0.)
Top startup-focused firms on Clutch list minimum project sizes of $10k-$50k at $25-$149/hr: Glorium $25k+, Cleveroad $10k+, Geniusee $50k+, Cheesecake Labs $50k+, Goji Labs $25k+; several offshore-priced firms at $25-$49/hr (Cleveroad, Geniusee, You Are Launched) compete for US startup MVP work. Nikolytics' price is 5-25x above the typical entry threshold a buyer sees on review sites. Caveats: minimums are self-reported, featured placement can be sponsored, and a $50k minimum doesn't stop those firms from delivering $250k projects.
Source: https://clutch.co/developers/startups

**Finding 6 — cold LinkedIn to US founders has quantified headwinds on all three funnel steps.** (Confidence: medium. Votes: 3-0, 3-0, 3-0.)
(a) Founders/owners are among the lowest-responding audiences: **6.4%** reply vs 10.9% for HR/Talent and 7.0% for C-level (Belkins, 14,077 contact-level records, 2025). (b) The US is the second-worst major market: **6.0%** reply vs Netherlands 10.5% (Belkins/Expandi, 5.3M US contacts). (c) Platform baselines from 13.2M connection requests (Expandi, May 2025-Apr 2026): **28.5%** connection acceptance, **3.0%** connection-note reply (declining to 2.2% by April 2026), **10.4%** post-connection message reply. Key limitation: all of it is automated, agency-run SDR outreach through Expandi's platform — not manual founder-to-founder DMs or voice notes. Treat these as the floor for cold agency outreach, not a forecast for a founder-led, content-supported approach, which this evidence does not test. Cross-corroborated by leadspark-ai, Cleverly, and PhantomBuster with no contradictions.
Sources: https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study | https://expandi.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks-2026/

**Finding 7 — the paid-scoping-with-credit mechanic is proven; $25k is the top of the range.** (Confidence: high. Votes: 3-0, 3-0.)
Sapient Codelabs sells a fixed-price 2-week Product Scoping Sprint at **$2,300** (offshore pricing — Surat, India, <$25/hr), "100% credited against the build", all deliverables client-owned, "no lock-in". WorkWise Solutions credits a **$30k** Discovery Sprint 100% toward a build. Lunch Pail Labs and Best Odds Corp use the same mechanic. So the mechanic is validated, with one comparable at $30k — but it competes against free scope calls (AppMakers) and 10x-cheaper offshore sprints. **No data surfaced anywhere on conversion rates from paid sprints to full builds** — that half of the research question is unanswered.
Sources: https://sapientcodelabs.com/scoping-sprint | https://workwisesolutions.org/faq/cost-and-roi/does-the-discovery-sprint-fee-credit-toward-a-build.html | https://lunchpaillabs.com/blog/paid-discovery-experiment | https://bestoddscorp.com/pricing | https://clutch.co/profile/sapient-codelabs

**Finding 8 — the demand premise is effectively unvalidated.** (Confidence: low. The meta-claim passed 3-0; the underlying demand claims were refuted 0-3.)
The only surviving statistic on outsourcing rates is Upsilon's uncited claim that "46% of businesses choose to outsource MVP development" (Dec 2024, published by a vendor selling MVP development, no study name, no link, no footnote — it circulates only in dev-shop marketing blogs). Every stronger demand claim failed verification: 59% offshore outsourcing, 20%+ CAGR market growth, the 87% developer-shortage trigger, founders-outsource-before-CTO — all refuted 0-3 or 1-2. There is **no verified evidence** on the rate at which funded seed-to-Series-B startups outsource core platform builds, or on the claimed purchase triggers.
Source: https://upsilon-it.medium.com/why-to-outsource-mvp-software-development-in-2025-1f5563d9e25d

### Three structural caveats (read before acting on any finding)

1. **Coverage gaps.** Questions 1 (demand rates/triggers) and 5 (how founders evaluate partners, objections, referral vs Clutch behavior) produced almost no surviving evidence, and question 7 survived only on the mechanic's existence, not conversion rates. The research is strongest on competition/pricing/positioning (questions 2-4) and weakest exactly where the plan is most exposed.
2. **Source quality skew.** Most competitive evidence is vendor marketing copy — fine for "what do competitors advertise", silent on what they actually deliver and to whom. Clutch figures are self-reported or review-skewed. LinkedIn benchmarks come from automation vendors, so they set a floor, not a forecast.
3. **Scope-tier conflation.** The crowding evidence ($4k-$25k studios, 14-day launches) sits one to two price tiers below a $250k funded-startup build. The positioning pillars are demonstrably occupied, but **no evidence surfaced of anyone delivering $250k-scope platforms live in 30 days** — the specific scope-speed-price combination was neither confirmed occupied nor confirmed deliverable.

Time sensitivity is high: note-reply rates dropped from 3.5% to 2.2% inside the Expandi measurement window, new studios appear monthly, and these mid-2026 figures may decay within quarters.

### What the research could not answer

- Conversion rates from paid discovery sprints ($5k-$30k) to full builds — and whether $25k raises or lowers downstream conversion versus free scoping. No data survived.
- The rate at which funded seed-to-Series-B US startups outsource **core** builds (vs MVPs or staff augmentation), and which triggers actually drive purchases. All quantitative claims refuted.
- How funded founders shortlist and vet dev partners in 2025-26 (referrals vs Clutch vs communities vs investor recommendations), and their top objections to a $250k fixed-price 30-day offer. Essentially unanswered.
- Whether anyone — including Nikolytics — can credibly deliver $250k-scope production platforms in 30 days, given the implied hours exceed a 4-6 person team's monthly capacity. If the delivery claim can be substantiated, this scope-speed combination is the real defensible whitespace.

### The five proposed amendments (pending your decision)

1. **Re-weight positioning from speed to certainty + post-MVP scope.** Speed is crowded; certainty at this scope is not.
2. **Ban "MVP" from all copy.** It anchors you to the $4k-$25k tier.
3. **Prioritize name candidate 3 ("Board-Proof") in the name test.**
4. **Make the first 100 Teardown conversations a structured demand-validation instrument** — the study the research couldn't find.
5. **Arm the Cheap Build Teardown with the research citations** (the findings above are its ammunition).

### The stack — $465,000 itemized

The full 65-problem list (before/during/after, bucketed by the four inverted value drivers) reduces to nine clusters. Every problem maps into exactly one stack item or bonus — one unaddressed perceived problem kills a $250k sale.

**1. The Boardroom Business Case — $15,000.** Problem: "I might be spending a quarter million on an annoyance, and my board will ask why we outsourced our core product." What they get: an ROI model tied to THEIR raise metrics (ARR, activation, pilot revenue); a loaded-cost comparison (two senior hires at $400k/yr plus 6 months of recruiting vs this); the **Cheap Build Teardown** — the documented reasons $60-80k offshore builds get rewritten, in board language; a board-deck-ready one-pager. If the verdict is "don't build," we say so, and the client keeps the analysis.

**2. The Head-to-Blueprint Sprint — $35,000.** Problem: "The product lives in my head; the flow I forget becomes the feature you skip." What they get: 2-3 structured extraction sessions run by the people who will actually build it; a written spec plus a clickable prototype of every core flow, signed before kickoff; an architecture document their technical advisor can audit, with stack choices justified in writing; the co-signed **Acceptance Checklist** that later powers both the guarantee and the final payment. Also sold standalone as **The Blueprint, $25,000**, 100% credited toward a build within 60 days, with waitlist priority — the defined entry offer and the natural downsell.

**3. The Locked-Number Charter — $25,000.** Problem: "Every fixed quote balloons; and if we freeze scope, we freeze my worst day-zero ideas." What they get: a contractual rule that any ambiguity inside the signed Blueprint resolves in the client's favor at no charge; **two Swap Tokens** — each replaces an unbuilt feature with a comparable one mid-build at zero cost, so week-two customer discovery improves the product instead of torching the budget; change orders exist only for net-new scope the client explicitly chooses.

**4. The Glass Box Build — $300,000.** Problem: "I wired six figures to people I cannot verify, building something I cannot see, on a timeline nobody else believes." What they get: **three senior engineers named in the contract with a no-substitution clause** (any change requires written approval); a **live staging URL from day 3, deployed daily** — click the real system anytime; a daily 3-minute end-of-day demo video; US business-hours overlap with blocker questions answered within 4 working hours; the **Integration War Room** — Stripe/Twilio/HubSpot sandboxes opened day one, the scariest third-party APIs attacked in week 1 while there's still slack; a milestone dashboard the board can see. Why 30 days is real: prebuilt, hardened infrastructure modules (auth, billing, admin, deploy pipelines) that we reuse and the client owns, so senior time goes into THEIR product, not boilerplate. Post-deposit leverage stays with the client: the work is visible daily, sits in their accounts (item 6), and on the split plan the final 20% is not due until they're live.
