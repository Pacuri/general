# Nikolytics Money Model | Built Around the Zero-Surprise Platform Sprint

Playbook 3 execution, final, 2026-07-03. Companion to flagship-offer.md (approved, fixed; nothing here re-decides it). Design emphasis: maximum 30-day collected cash per client, scored with honest accounting: full costs booked before any pass is claimed, and cash that lands after day 30 is dated, never smuggled into the 30-day bar.

**Targets set before any play was chosen (Playbook 3, Step 1; money-models.md, Core concepts).** Minimum bar: 30-day collected cash from one client exceeds CAC plus fulfillment. $100M bar: one client's 30-day profit funds getting AND servicing at least two more. Planning assumptions, conservative ends of every stated range: warm CAC $5,000; flagship fulfillment $35,000 all-in ($30,000 team cost plus $5,000 audit and bonus-delivery reserve); Blueprint fulfillment $12,000; Iteration Week fulfillment $8,000; Extended Shield servicing reserve $5,000 per year; Engine cycle fulfillment $4,000. At warm CAC the $100M threshold is 2 x $40,000 = **$80,000** of 30-day profit per client. At paid CAC the bar moves with CAC, so Section 7 derives the real ceiling instead of assuming the $66k-$73k allowable band clears it. Bootstrapped rule in force everywhere: every customer acquired at a profit, no plan spends uncollected cash, prepaid delivery is reserved the day cash lands.

## 1. The Model at a Glance | Ascension Map, First Touch to Year Two

| Step | Offer | Price | When | Type and source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Blueprint** | $25,000 | First touch, or the ladder's terminus | Attraction: Decoy Offer (money-models.md, Attraction Offers §3) |
| 2 | **Zero-Surprise Platform Sprint** (fixed, approved) | $250,000 | Presented beside the Blueprint; Blueprint credits 100% within 60 days | Core Grand Slam Offer (flagship-offer.md) |
| 3 | **The Commitment Ladder** | terms change, price never | On any flagship "no" | Downsell: Payment Plan + Feature Downsell alternating (money-models.md, Downsell Offers §1 and §3) |
| 4 | **The Traction Block** | $80,000 prepaid | Revealed at the day-14 staging demo, closed at the Launch Plan Review (~day 25) | Upsell: Classic Upsell with the Economist bundle play (money-models.md, Upsell Offers §1; §2 'Economist') |
| 5 | **The Product Engine** | $16,000 per 4-week cycle | Handoff meeting at go-live (~day 34), placed LAST | Continuity: Continuity Bonus (money-models.md, Continuity Offers §1) |
| 6 | Engine bulk prepay: 5 cycles + 1 free | $80,000 | At Engine signup | Placement-doctrine prepaid-bulk play (money-models.md, Continuity Offers) |
| Year 2 | Evergreen year-two renewal, Engine annual terms, Founding Partner conversations | | Month 10+ | LTV layer, outside the 30-day engine |

One customer's maximum path, honestly dated: $250,000 PIF at day 0 plus $80,000 Traction Block at day ~25 = **$330,000 collected inside 30 days**; the Engine's $16,000 first cycle or $80,000 bulk prepay lands at the day ~34 handoff, taking the total to **$410,000 by day 35**. Day-31+ cash is real money and it is never scored against the 30-day bar. Compounding rationale: 2x value x 2x customers x 2x speed = 8x (money-models.md, Core concepts). Every close in this model happens inside a meeting that already exists for delivery (Blueprint Debrief, day-14 demo, Launch Plan Review, handoff), so a founder-operated front end can run it with zero sales team.

**The capacity spine (constraint 7, stated once, enforced everywhere):** 3 concurrent build slots. One shared post-launch feature-week lane, maximum **one team-week per month across the whole business**, exactly as the approved offer doc fixes it; every post-launch week (PIF Momentum Sprint bonus, Block Iteration Weeks, Engine Burn-Down bonus) draws from this lane through a published **Week Ledger**. The ledger caps outstanding owed weeks at **6**; when it is full, Block and Burn-Down sales pause and prospects get a numbered, dated waitlist, the same honesty mechanic as build slots. The lane rises to two weeks per month only when a fourth senior build team is hired. Tenth seat (the named maintenance engineer): Evergreen capped at 6 active plans (approved), Extended Shield capped at **4 active** until a second named maintenance hire. The Product Engine does not launch until a dedicated iteration pair is hired (Section 5). The Block is therefore an inventory-limited offer by design; its scarcity is real and published, never theatrical.

**Win Your Money Back gate, addressed and declined (constraint 6):** WYMB requires a measured baseline refund rate under 5%. Nikolytics has no refund history to certify the gate, the approved guarantee is conditional service-based with no refund mechanism, and a platform build is not a start-and-quit product. WYMB is excluded on the gate, not on taste; the rollover-credit mechanic it needs already lives in the Blueprint's 100% credit.

## 2. Attraction Offer Spec | The Blueprint

**Source: money-models.md, Attraction Offers §3, Decoy Offer; credit mechanics per Upsell Offers §4, Rollover.** Advertise the accessible thing, present it beside the premium thing, either purchase wins.

- **What:** the approved $25,000 standalone spec + prototype sprint (stack item 2 sold alone): extraction sessions, co-signed spec, clickable prototype, auditable architecture document, Acceptance Checklist. 100% credited toward a build signed within 60 days; waitlist priority. Nothing re-decided; this is the flagship's own entry offer given its formal money-model seat.
- **Why a paid decoy, not free:** discount offers pull fewer leads but higher show rates, and no-shows are expensive when a senior pair delivers the sprint (Decoy free-vs-discount rule). $25,000 also pre-qualifies for a $250,000 conversation.
- **The decoy construction:** stripped of the build, the Live-in-30 Guarantee, the bonuses, and the warranty. On the call, premium first: "Are you here for a document or a launched platform?" Most funded founders answer platform; present the Sprint with the Blueprint as the safety rung. Burger economics keep the roles straight: the Blueprint is the $2.00 burger (~$13,000 gross profit); the build is where profit lives (~$215,000 gross profit). The thing sold most is never where the money is.
- **Credit math (Rollover rule):** $250,000 / $25,000 = 10x, an effective 10% discount on conversion, well inside the 25% ceiling. The 60-day expiry is the honest urgency.
- **Cash and timing:** collected 100% at signing; Blueprint kickoffs ride the first-and-third-Monday cohort calendar. Fulfillment $8k-$12k, senior pair, ~1 week.
- **Operating rule (worksheet-derived, Section 7):** schedule Blueprint clients so **Debrief-to-kickoff is 7 days or less**, or close PIF at the Debrief. This single scheduling rule is what keeps Scenario C above the $100M bar.
- **Capacity:** Blueprints consume pair-weeks, not build slots; cap 2 concurrent Blueprints so they never starve a live build. Track from day one: **at least 60% of Blueprints convert to builds inside the 60-day window** is the health target.

## 3. Upsell Offer Spec | The Traction Block

**Source: money-models.md, Upsell Offers §1, Classic Upsell (bundle + name; charge for guarantees/warranties at +5-50%), priced with the §2 Menu Upsell 'Economist' play.**

**The revealed problem (problem-solution cycle):** the day-14 staging demo is the first time the founder clicks their real product, and it reliably manufactures the next problem: a feature list the two Swap Tokens cannot absorb, plus the realization that at go-live the free coverage is bugs (Shield, 60 days) and patches (Evergreen, 12 months), not new features. You cannot put a live platform in front of real users without an iteration engine.

**Timing, fixed and stated once:** planted at the day-14 demo ("everyone asks about post-launch feature weeks; we will price it at the Launch Plan Review"), then offered and closed at the **Launch Plan Review**, a scheduled meeting at ~day 25 that already exists to choreograph Launch Command. This lands the cash inside the 30-day window without stretching anything, and it deliberately separates the $80,000 ask from any payment event: no client ever hears an upsell minutes after wiring their final 20%.

**The bundle, one named offer:**
1. **Two Iteration Weeks** | full build-speed post-launch feature weeks, booked from the published Week Ledger, valid 12 months. Standalone price $40,000 per week. The paid week carries one deliverable the free Momentum Sprint bonus (stated value $35,000, unchanged) does not: a written post-week impact report and roadmap session. One public price, one honest $5,000 delta, no competing valuations.
2. **The Extended Shield** | the paid-warranty play: defect coverage on code we wrote, running **12 months from the day the client's free Shield expires**, whether that is day 61 or day 181 (when the guarantee extended it). It never overlaps a single free day. 1-business-day SLA, serviced by the tenth seat. Standalone price $25,000 (10% of build price, inside the +5-50% band).

**The Economist pricing:** A = Extended Shield, $25,000. B = two Iteration Weeks, $80,000. C = both together, $80,000. C equals B, so C is the obvious buy, prepaid in full. Cardinal rule holds: nothing discounts the flagship or the weeks; the sharpening is added value, never subtracted price.

**BAMFAM chain:** signing books the day-14 demo; the demo books the Launch Plan Review and plants the Block; the review closes the Block and books the go-live walkthrough; go-live books Growth Architecture Session 1; Session 1 books the handoff meeting where continuity lives.

**PIF interaction:** Full-Send buyers already hold one free Momentum week, so their Block is positioned as weeks two and three. The paid offer extends the bonus, never devalues it.

**Capacity honesty:** every Block enters the Week Ledger at sale (two owed weeks) and is budgeted the day cash lands. Blocks are sold only while the ledger shows open capacity inside the buyer's 12-month validity; at the 6-owed-week cap, sales pause publicly. At the one-week-per-month lane this means roughly 4-6 Blocks per year alongside PIF bonuses until the fourth-team hire; the worksheet scenarios in Section 7 are per-client paths, not simultaneous promises to every client.

## 4. Downsell Ladder | The Commitment Ladder

**Source: money-models.md, Downsell Offers §1 Payment Plan (7-step ladder, most cash up front first) and §3 Feature Downsell, alternated per the sequencing rule; temperature check routes.** Cardinal rule everywhere: never the same thing for less. Two contractual floors: the $250,000 price and the 54% signing deposit. Trial With Penalty is excluded (no $0 starts against those floors; this is not a recurring do-the-work product). The approved terms already ARE rung one: prepay presented as the rewarded path ($85,000 value incentive), never plans punished. From the first "no":

1. **Rung 1, financing rails (payment-plan step 2):** "Would you rather I decide your payment terms, or you decide?" Third-party financing or their corporate card pays Nikolytics 100% at signing while the client self-finances. Because Nikolytics is genuinely paid in full at signing, financed-full buyers receive the Full-Send incentives, no dilution, that IS payment in full at signing. This rung is the single biggest cash-velocity lever in the model: it converts a terms objection into a functional PIF.
2. **Rung 2, the Builder's Split** as approved: 54% / 26% / 20%, deposit reason stated openly. Never softened.
3. **Temperature check:** "1 to 10, how badly do you want this platform live in 30 days?" **8+** routes to timing structures; **7 or below** asks "what would make it a 10?" and routes to features.
4. **8+ terminus, Reserve with the Blueprint:** for the funded founder whose round closes in six weeks, pay $25,000 today, lock waitlist priority and the 60-day credit, and BAMFAM the build signing to a named date tied to the round close with the kickoff cohort pre-reserved. We move the kickoff date, never the deposit and never past go-live for the final 20%. A hot prospect gets closed, not parked.
5. **≤7 terminus, feature downsell, the Blueprint:** a genuinely different thing for less, $25,000, honoring its approved role as the natural downsell, with built-in urgency (credit expires day 60) and a scheduled cohort Monday booked before they leave the call. Both termini deposit the prospect at the top of the ascension map, not outside it.
6. **Rescue rung (free-orientation pattern):** for a hard no, a complimentary Cheap Build Teardown briefing against their current vendor quotes. Booked, not open-ended; it sells nothing and schedules the next conversation. Then the nurture list and the pre-kickoff check-in.

**Health metric:** the count of paid-in-full closes (including financed-full) must hold after the ladder goes live. If PIF count drops, full payers are leaking into the split; re-script the rung order before touching anything else.

## 5. Continuity Offer Spec | The Product Engine (placed LAST)

**Source: money-models.md, Continuity Offers §1, Continuity Bonus, plus the placement doctrine and bulk-prepay math.**

**Placement:** offered at the handoff meeting at go-live, ~day 34 on the standard calendar, after attraction, upsell, and downsell have all run, exactly per doctrine. The first payment lands just past the 30-day window and is scored there; the book is explicit that the right timing after day 30 beats forcing (money-models.md, build procedure step 4). The Engine is day-35 cash and this document says so.

**Fit test and the three-way scope wall (constraint 3):** ongoing value for ongoing payments, with a hard split so nothing double-sells free coverage:
- **Founders' Shield / Extended Shield:** defects in code we wrote (free to day 60 or 180; paid for 12 months after, via the Block).
- **Evergreen Codebase Plan:** dependency updates and security patching, free 12 months (24 on PIF).
- **The Product Engine:** net-new feature development and growth work only, which no free bonus and no warranty touches. Not a payment plan in disguise: stop paying, the work stops, nothing already owed keeps billing.
- **The fold-in (year-two answer):** when the free Evergreen term expires (month 13, or month 25 for PIF clients), its maintenance scope folds into an active Engine at no price change. Additive by scope now, successor by timing later; the Engine never charges for anything the client already holds free.

**What $16,000 per 4-week cycle buys:** three senior dev-days of roadmap-driven feature work delivered by the dedicated iteration pair, a monthly iteration-roadmap session (delivered by the team; it begins where the four Growth Architecture Sessions with Nikola end, never replacing them), priority booking rights on the Week Ledger, and ROI Instrumentation Pack reviews so the board slide stays current.

**The Continuity Bonus close (advertise the bonus, not the membership):** join at handoff and receive **The Backlog Burn-Down Week**, one full-speed feature week, standalone value $40,000, scheduled through the Week Ledger. The bonus is worth 2.5x the first payment, and the standalone alternative (a single Iteration Week at $40,000) is 2.5x the first cycle. The book's tested table maps 2.33x-2.66x standalone pricing to roughly 80-90% choosing continuity; 2.5x sits inside that band, and it is a planning hypothesis, not data, until the first five handoffs are instrumented. Commitment trade: the Burn-Down Week attaches only to a 6-cycle minimum.

**Bulk prepay recovery play:** immediately after the Engine yes: "prepay 5 cycles, get the 6th free," $80,000 collected at handoff. Book rule of thumb: 1-in-8 uptake on this class of play lifts 30-day profit ~50%. Capped at half of active Engine clients at any time (the 10%-of-base cap adapted to a small client count) to protect recurring cash.

**Cancellation policy, decided now:** cancellation fee equals the bonus value consumed (the Burn-Down Week's $40,000 if taken early), the cancellation channel is easy to find, an exit interview waives the fee, and an "I wanted more" complaint routes to a Rollover conversation into a bigger Engine tier or the Founding Partner tier. Note: the Founding Partner anchor keeps its approved $1.5M price and framing; this model deliberately declines any rollover credit against it, because the anchor's price is an approved element, not a money-model variable.

**Capacity honesty:** the Engine launches only after a dedicated iteration pair is hired (Q2 2027 gate) and is capped at **4 active clients** on that pair's capacity; Burn-Down bonus weeks enter the Week Ledger like every other owed week. Tenth-seat load (6 Evergreen + 4 Extended Shield caps) is published and rises only with a second named maintenance hire.

## 6. Revenue Micro-Plays, Each Tagged to Its Rollout Quarter

**Sources: money-models.md, Continuity Offers §2 micro-plays; Payment Plan operating tactics; Rollover Upsell.** Tagged so a four-person team never runs a play before its quarter.

1. **Prepay-as-rewarded framing (Q3 2026):** every terms conversation opens with the financed framing, then prepay presented as the rewarded path (the approved $85k incentive). Same math, friendlier anchor.
2. **+3% processing fee, waived for a second payment method (Q3 2026):** on a $250k card or financed transaction that is $7,500 protected; waived for ACH or wire on file, the correct B2B rail, which also fixes the expired-card leak on recurring charges later.
3. **Mid-plan balance clear (Q4 2026):** at the day-14 milestone, split-plan clients get one scripted reminder: clear the remaining 46% ($115,000) today and a **fifth Growth Architecture Session with Nikola attaches** (stated value $7,500, an added deliverable extending the approved four-session bonus, never a Full-Send item). Every Full-Send incentive, Momentum Sprint and Evergreen doubling included, stays exclusive to true at-signing payment in full, so "at signing" is never diluted; the balance-clear incentive is deliberately smaller and named as such. Each jump pulls $115,000 forward.
4. **Declined-payment recovery (Q4 2026):** milestone and Engine charges scheduled to the client's stated payment-ops day; declines re-run the same day (recovers roughly a third).
5. **VC gift certificates (Q1 2027):** transferable $5,000 Blueprint certificates (face = 20% of the $25,000 Blueprint, per the gift-card sizing rule) given to investor partners for portfolio companies, usable only by someone other than the holder. A referral engine that costs nothing until it works.
6. **Blueprint revival winback (Q1 2027):** lapsed Blueprint holders whose 60-day credit expired get one personalized-video revival: 50% of the Blueprint ($12,500) re-credited toward a build signed by a named cohort date. $250k ≥ 4 x $12.5k, price floor untouched, and the deadline stays honest because the full credit stayed dead.
7. **4-week Engine billing (Q2 2027):** 13 cycles vs 12 months = +8.3% revenue; "$16,000 every 4 weeks" converts like "$16,000 a month."
8. **Lifetime rate lock (post-Engine, gated on data):** a permanent locked cycle rate earned just past the measured average drop-off cycle. Placeholder until real churn exists; never launched on invented data.
9. **Year-two Evergreen renewal (month 10+):** paid Evergreen year two offered with a prepay incentive. LTV layer, outside the 30-day engine.

## 7. The 30-Day Cash Worksheet | Three Scenarios, Both Bars, Full Costs

Assumptions restated from the preamble: CAC $5,000 warm; flagship fulfillment $35,000 all-in; Blueprint $12,000; Block delivery reserve $21,000 (two weeks at $8,000 plus $5,000 Extended Shield servicing); Engine cycle $4,000. Every scenario books full CAC and full fulfillment before claiming a pass; cash landing after day 30 is shown but never scored against the 30-day bar. Warm $100M bar: profit ≥ $80,000.

**Scenario A | PIF flagship.** Sign Thursday day 0, kickoff Monday day 4, demo day 18, Launch Plan Review ~day 25, go-live day 34.
- Base: $250,000 collected day 0. Costs $5,000 + $35,000 = $40,000. **Profit $210,000.** Minimum bar: pass. $100M bar: 2.6x the $80,000 requirement; funds getting and servicing **5** more clients. **Pass.**
- With the Block closed at the day-25 review: **$330,000 collected inside 30 days**; costs $40,000 + $21,000 Block reserve = $61,000; **profit $269,000**, 3.4x. Day ~34 handoff adds the Engine ($16,000 cycle 1, or $80,000 bulk prepay with $24,000 of cycle delivery reserved), reaching **$410,000 by day 35**, dated as such.
- **Paid-CAC governor, derived not assumed:** the $100M bar solves to **CAC ≤ ~$48,000 on the naked build** (215,000 - CAC ≥ 2 x (CAC + 35,000)) and **CAC ≤ ~$68,000 with the Block attached** (274,000 - CAC ≥ 2 x (CAC + 35,000)). At a flat $70,000 CAC even the with-Block path narrowly fails ($204,000 vs a $210,000 bar). Consequence: the minimum bar passes across the whole $66k-$73k allowable band, but **paid spend paces to the derived $48k-$68k governor, not to the 3:1 allowable ceiling**, and Stage II must be live and attaching before any paid scaling.

**Scenario B | Split-plan flagship.** Same calendar: $135,000 at signing (day 0), $65,000 at the day-18 milestone, $50,000 at go-live day ~34, excluded conservatively.
- Inside 30 days: **$200,000 collected.** Costs $40,000. **Profit $160,000.** Minimum bar: pass. $100M bar: 2.0x; funds **4** more clients. **Pass.** The 54% front-load is doing exactly the client-financed-acquisition job the flagship doc assigned it.
- With the Block at the day-25 review: **$280,000 collected**, costs $61,000, **profit $219,000**, 2.7x. With a mid-plan balance clear at day 18 as well: up to $395,000 collected (the attached fifth Growth Architecture Session costs Nikola-hours, not a team-week, so nothing new enters the Week Ledger).
- Paid-CAC governor: base split passes the $100M bar only to **CAC ≤ ~$32,000**; with the Block, **CAC ≤ ~$51,000**. Split closes at high paid CAC do not clear the bar alone, which is why the PIF path is always presented first and the financing rung exists: the ladder's job is to keep the paid-CAC book weighted toward functional PIF.

**Scenario C | Blueprint-first client.** Blueprint day 0, Debrief ~day 10, build signed at the Debrief on the split, kickoff day ~14 (the ≤7-day rule), day-14 milestone lands day ~28.
- C1 conservative (milestone excluded): $25,000 + ($135,000 signing minus $25,000 credit = $110,000) = **$135,000 collected.** Full costs booked up front: $5,000 + $12,000 + $35,000 = $52,000. **Profit $83,000.** Minimum bar: pass. $100M bar: **narrow pass** ($83,000 vs $80,000), and it is narrow, which is exactly why the ≤7-day scheduling rule exists. With the milestone inside day 30: $200,000 collected, profit $148,000, 1.85x. PIF at the Debrief: $250,000 collected, profit $198,000, 2.5x.
- C2, no conversion yet: $25,000 - $17,000 = **profit $8,000.** Minimum bar: pass, the decoy pays for its own acquisition and delivery. $100M bar: not its job; the 60-day credit expiry and waitlist priority force the conversion decision, and the ≥60% conversion target (Section 2) is the number that keeps this branch honest.

**Blended LTGP for the paid-ads floor, attach rates stated:** build gross profit ~$215,000; at a planning 50% Block attach (+~$29,500) and 50% Engine attach at the 6-cycle minimum (+~$36,000), blended LTGP ≈ **$280,000**, giving 4:1 at a $70,000 CAC. The 3:1 floor is therefore not the binding constraint; the 30-day governor above is, and it is the number that gates Q1 2027 spend.

Bootstrapped rule honored in all three scenarios: every path acquires the customer at a profit inside 30 days, the tight first three weeks are covered because the 54% deposit or full PIF collects before fulfillment spend peaks, and every prepaid week or cycle is reserved the day it is collected.

## 8. Grand Slam Completion Check

The Grand Slam definition requires a money model that pays you to acquire customers (offers.md §2, via Playbook 3 step 9). Verdict: the 54% front-load alone finances two more acquisitions at warm CAC; PIF finances five; PIF plus Block finances six. Terms, the conditional service guarantee (doubling as the money-model backstop, since the final 20% gates on go-live and creates zero refund exposure), premium price, and the unmatchable stack were approved in flagship-offer.md and are untouched here. Simple scales, fancy fails: four named offers, one product family, every close inside an already-scheduled delivery meeting, 100 ways to offer one product rather than 100 products. The ladder never sells the same thing for less, continuity never resells a bonus, every cap (slots, ledger, Evergreen, Extended Shield, Engine) is numeric and published, and no bar is ever passed with out-of-window cash.

## 9. Rollout Order | One Offer Perfected Per Quarter

Mapped to the five-step evolution (money-models.md, Building your Money Model). Nothing launches finished from zero; cash is tight until late July, so Q3 spends nothing that has not collected. Each quarter has one offer to perfect and a gate to pass.

- **Q3 2026 | Stage I, Get Cash (evolution steps 1-2).** Warm outreach and referral only. Blueprint decoy as the advertised front door, flagship presented beside it, ladder scripted but used only through the financing rung. Micro-plays 1-2 live in contracts from day one. **Gate: two builds closed and collected, PIF share tracked.**
- **Q4 2026 | Stage II part 1, Get More Cash (step 3).** Traction Block planted at every day-14 demo and closed at every Launch Plan Review within Week Ledger capacity; Extended Shield caps live; mid-plan balance clear and decline-recovery running. **Gate: Block offered on every eligible build, ledger never oversold, PIF count holding.**
- **Q1 2027 | Stage II part 2 (steps 3-4).** Full ladder in every conversation, financing partner live, winback and VC-certificate plays running, organic content feeding the pipeline. First paid-ads tests only if the derived CAC governor ($48k naked, $68k with Block) and the 2x-collected test-budget rule both hold. **Gate: 30-day cash per client sustaining test spend at a measured per-channel CAC.**
- **Q2 2027 | Stage III, Get Most Cash (steps 4-5).** Iteration pair hired, then the Product Engine launches at every handoff with the Burn-Down bonus and bulk prepay; 4-week billing live; second maintenance hire gates Evergreen and Shield cap raises; fourth-team hire gates the week lane rising to two per month. Only after the Engine is reliable does spend push toward the governor. Build ops in parallel: when the money model starts working, the business starts breaking.
